### [1] [Improving Medical Communication using Rubric-Guided Counterfactual Recommendations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18889)
*Adrian Cosma,Nicoleta-Nina Basoc,Andrei Niculae,Cosmin Dumitrache,Emilian Radoi*

Main category: cs.CL

TL;DR: This paper proposes a rubric-guided counterfactual recommendation pipeline based on language models to improve doctor-patient text communication in telemedicine, which recommends small interpretable changes to communication style without altering medical content to increase positive patient feedback.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Text-based telemedicine relies on patient feedback that mainly reflects perceived communication quality rather than medical accuracy. There is a need for methods that can improve communication quality to get better patient feedback without interfering with the original medical content of doctors' responses.

Method: An LM-guided counterfactual recommendation pipeline is introduced. It first discovers and refines interpretable communication features including tone, personalization, actionability, and completeness. It uses these features along with interaction metadata to estimate positive patient feedback. At inference, it searches for low-cost ordinal feature changes and recommends minimal communication adjustments, and independent auditor models are used to validate whether the performance gains generalize.

Result: Under the evaluation of independent auditors, the proposed recommendations achieve a mean gain of +6.41% in predicted positive feedback probability, and 93.31% of the recommendations have non-negative gain in predicted feedback.

Conclusion: Small, interpretable communication changes can capture most predicted gains in positive patient feedback while preserving doctors' control over medical reasoning and final wording of responses.

Abstract: Text-based telemedicine increasingly relies on lightweight patient feedback, however, such feedback primarily reflects perceived communication quality rather than medical accuracy. We introduce an LM-guided counterfactual recommendation pipeline that discovers and refines interpretable communication features such as tone, personalization, actionability and completeness in addressing patient concerns, without interfering with the medical content. These features are used together with patient-doctor interaction metadata to estimate positive feedback. At inference time, the system searches over low-cost ordinal feature changes and recommends minimal communication changes predicted to increase the probability of positive feedback, while independent auditor models test whether these gains generalize beyond the selection model. Across interactions, recommendations yield a mean +6.41% gain in predicted positive feedback probability under independent auditors, and are non-negative for 93.31% of recommendations. These results suggest that small, interpretable communication changes can capture most predicted gains while preserving the doctor's control over medical reasoning and final wording.

### [2] [RECOM: A Validity Discrimination Tradeoff in Automatic Metrics for Open Ended Reddit Question Answering](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19218)
*Pushwitha Krishnappa,Amit Das,Vinija Jain,Aman Chadha,Tathagata Mukherjee*

Main category: cs.CL

TL;DR: This paper identifies a tradeoff between validity (distinguishing genuine content alignment from surface overlap) and discriminative power (ranking different models by performance) for automatic evaluation metrics of large language model (LLM)-generated open-ended question answering. It introduces the contamination-free RECOM dataset of 15,000 r/AskReddit questions with authentic post-cutoff community replies, evaluates multiple automatic metrics and LLM-as-judge on this dataset, and finds all metrics fail to perform well on both axes, recommending joint reporting of performance on both with an explicit random baseline.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Automatic metrics are widely used to evaluate LLM-generated text, but they are required to simultaneously achieve two goals: validity (separating genuine content alignment from surface coincidence) and discriminative power (distinguishing better models from worse ones). This work explores whether these two goals conflict in open-ended, opinion-driven question answering, a gap that has not been systematically studied with a contamination-free evaluation setup.

Method: The authors construct RECOM, a contamination-free evaluation dataset consisting of 15,000 r/AskReddit questions collected from September 2025, paired with authentic community replies that postdate all evaluated models' training cutoffs. They evaluate multiple common automatic metrics (cosine similarity, BERTScore) and three independent LLM judges on five open-source 7-10B LLMs, comparing metric performance against a random-derangement noise floor to separately measure validity and discriminative power, controlling for confounding factors like response length.

Result: No evaluated metric performs well on both validity and discriminative power: Cosine similarity achieves strong validity (Cohen's d ≈ 2, effectively separating real from random answers) but almost no ability to rank models (|d| < 0.1). BERTScore precision appears to rank models well before controlling for length (raw |d| up to 0.63), but after length control, its ranking ability collapses to |d| = 0.09, and it also has much weaker validity (d ≈ 0.8) than cosine similarity. This tradeoff is a property of the metrics themselves (not the evaluated models) rooted in representation design, and independent LLM judges also only weakly separate the five models and reproduce the validity gap.

Conclusion: There is an inherent validity-discrimination tradeoff in existing automatic metrics for open-ended opinion-driven question answering. The paper recommends that researchers report metric performance on both validity and discriminative axes, alongside an explicit random-baseline noise floor. The RECOM dataset is publicly released to support future research on LLM evaluation.

Abstract: Automatic metrics are the default for evaluating LLM-generated text, yet a metric is quietly asked to do two jobs: tell genuine content alignment from surface coincidence (validity), and tell a better system from a worse one (discriminative power). On open-ended, opinion-driven question answering, the two are in tension. We introduce RECOM (Reddit Evaluation for Correspondence of Models), a contamination-free evaluation dataset of 15,000 r/AskReddit questions (September 2025), each paired with its authentic community replies, which postdate every evaluated model's training cutoff. Scoring five open-source LLMs (7--10B) against every reply each metric paired with a random-derangement noise floor we find that no metric does both jobs well. Cosine similarity separates real from random answers (Cohen's $d \approx 2$) but cannot rank the five models ($|d| < 0.1$); BERTScore precision appears to rank the models (raw $|d|$ up to 0.63), but once response length is controlled this collapses to $|d| = 0.09$ and its validity is weak ($d \approx 0.8$, versus cosine's $\approx 2$). Because every metric scores the same outputs, this validity--discrimination tradeoff is a property of the metrics, not the models, and we argue it stems from representation design. Three independent LLM judges reproduce the validity gap and likewise separate the five models only weakly. We recommend reporting metrics on both axes, with an explicit random-baseline floor. RECOM is publicly available at https://anonymous.4open.science/r/recom-D4B0

### [3] [CAPRA: Scaling Feedback on Software Architecture Deliverables with a Multi-Agent LLM System](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18976)
*Marco Becattini,Niccolò Caselli,Matteo Minin,Roberto Verdecchia,Enrico Vicario*

Main category: cs.AI

TL;DR: 本文提出了一个多代理大语言模型系统CAPRA，用于自动化分析软件架构交付物并生成符合模板的个性化LaTeX反馈，经初步评估在学生报告上表现良好，可扩展架构交付物反馈流程。


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: 软件工程教育中，代码评分和作文评分的自动化评估已有显著进展，但需要分析结构完整性和需求可追溯性的软件架构交付物评审尚未完全自动化，将大语言模型应用于该任务需要可靠架构来保证技术反馈的准确性。

Method: 设计CAPRA多代理LLM系统，协调多个专用智能体，基于Python微服务结合PyMuPDF和视觉LLM（gpt-4o）提取多模态文档中的文本和UML图；引入基于归一化莱文斯坦距离模糊匹配的确定性证据锚定步骤，通过一致性管理智能体交叉验证、去重和合并结果，使用包含八项标准的二元评估分类法对系统性能进行评估。

Result: 对10份学生报告的初步评估显示，在严格的双评分者聚合规则下，CAPRA满足了88.8%的评估标准，与人类评估者达到中等评分者间一致性（kappa=0.582），每份报告处理时间略超过4分钟。

Conclusion: 结果支持LLM支持的架构反馈方案的可行性，但对于主观评估维度，人工监督仍然必不可少。

Abstract: Automated assessment in software engineering education has advanced significantly for code grading and essay scoring. However, reviewing software architecture deliverables, which requires analyzing structural completeness and requirements traceability, has not yet been fully automated. Applying Large Language Models (LLMs) to this task requires robust architectures to ensure technical feedback is accurate and reliable for students. This paper presents CAPRA (Configurable Architecture Proficiency Report Assessment), a multi-agent LLM system that analyzes software architecture deliverables to generate personalized, template-compliant LaTeX feedback. As a core design choice, CAPRA coordinates multiple specialized agents and employs a Python-based microservice for multi-modal document extraction, utilizing PyMuPDF and vision-enabled LLMs (specifically gpt-4o) to parse text and UML diagrams. To ensure educational reliability and mitigate hallucinations, CAPRA introduces a deterministic Evidence Anchoring step using fuzzy matching via normalized Levenshtein distance, along with a ConsistencyManager agent that cross-verifies, deduplicates, and merges findings. System performance is assessed using a structured eight-criterion binary evaluation taxonomy covering: (i) extraction completeness, (ii) feature validation, (iii) issue grounding and severity detection, (iv) recommendation specificity and traceability, and (v) template and tone compliance. A preliminary empirical evaluation on 10 student reports shows that CAPRA satisfied 88.8% of the evaluated criteria under a strict two-rater aggregation rule, achieved moderate inter-rater agreement with human evaluators (kappa = 0.582), and processed each report in slightly over 4 minutes. While these results support the viability of LLM-supported architectural feedback, human oversight remains essential for subjective assessment dimensions.

### [4] [SAERec: Constructing Fine-grained Interpretable Intents Priors via Sparse Autoencoders for Recommendation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18897)
*Jiangnan Xia,Xuansheng Wu,Yu Yang,Xin Wang,Ninghao Liu*

Main category: cs.IR

TL;DR: 本文提出了面向意图推荐的模型SAERec，它利用稀疏自动编码器从大语言模型的文本嵌入中提取细粒度可解释意图，将其作为先验引导推荐，在公开数据集上性能超过现有最优模型，同时具备可解释性。


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: 现有基于意图的推荐模型大多直接从用户序列通过聚类或原型学习推导意图，存在对序列质量敏感、需要预设意图数量、缺乏显式语义 grounding 的问题，导致意图集合不完整、粒度粗，限制了推荐效果。

Method: 1. 使用稀疏自动编码器对大语言模型输出的文本嵌入解耦，从文本语料中提取全面的细粒度可解释意图集合，分离出意图相关语义和文本噪声；2. 为每个用户从意图集合中检索相关意图作为先验，包含匹配用户当前兴趣的个人意图和捕获通用物品模式的公共意图；3. 设计多分支注意力机制捕获时序依赖并注入个人和公共意图信号，再通过自适应融合层构建最终用户表示用于推荐。

Result: 在多个公开数据集上的大量实验表明，SAERec consistently优于最先进的基线模型，同时能够提供人类可理解的推荐解释。

Conclusion: SAERec能够从文本语料自动构建细粒度、可解释的意图空间来引导推荐，解决了现有意图推荐方法的诸多缺陷，在推荐准确率和可解释性上都取得更好表现。

Abstract: Intent-based recommender systems have gained significant attention for improving accuracy and interpretability by modeling the underlying motivations behind user behaviors. Most existing models derive intents directly from user sequences via clustering or prototype learning. However, they are sensitive to sequence quality, require presetting the number of intents, and lack explicit semantic grounding. These issues lead to an incomplete and coarse intent set and limit the effectiveness of recommendation. In this paper, we propose the Sparse Autoencoder for intent-based recommendation (SAERec), a novel recommender that automatically constructs a fine-grained and interpretable intent space from a textual corpus to guide recommendation. Rather than treating texts as side signals, SAERec leverages them as high information density evidence for intent construction. Specifically, we first extract a comprehensive set of fine-grained interpretable intents from the latent space of large language models (LLMs) by using a sparse autoencoder (SAE) to disentangle and interpret text embeddings, which isolates intent-related semantics from textual noise. Then, for each user, we retrieve relevant intents from this set as priors to guide recommendation. It contains personal intents matching a user's current interests and public intents capturing general item patterns shared across users (e.g., quality, price). Finally, to integrate retrieved intents into sequence modeling, we propose a multi-branch attention mechanism that captures temporal dependencies and injects both personal and public intent signals, followed by an adaptive fusion layer to construct the final user representation for recommendation. Extensive experiments on public datasets demonstrate the superiority of SAERec, consistently outperforming state-of-the-art baselines while providing human-understandable explanations.

### [6] [Beyond Algorithms: Conceptual Innovation in Medical Imaging AI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19270)
*Mark A. Anastasio*

Main category: cs.LG

TL;DR: 这是一篇观点性文章，指出当前医学成像人工智能领域存在重算法创新、轻概念创新的不平衡问题，区分了两种创新类型，分析了现有体系对概念创新的低估及其负面影响，并给出了促进行业平衡发展的行动建议。


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>

Abstract: Artificial intelligence has driven rapid progress in medical imaging research, producing increasingly sophisticated algorithms and steady improvements on benchmark tasks.
