### [1] [Large Language Models (LLMs) and Generative AI in Cybersecurity and Privacy: A Survey of Dual-Use Risks, AI-Generated Malware, Explainability, and Defensive Strategies](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06963)
*Kiarash Ahi,Saeed Valizadeh*

Main category: cs.CL

TL;DR: This paper is a comprehensive survey that reviews over 70 sources to examine both defensive and malicious dual-use of large language models (LLMs) and generative AI in cybersecurity, covering key topics including AI-generated malware, explainability, existing defensive practices, and provides actionable recommendations and a research roadmap for secure LLM deployment in cybersecurity.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: LLMs and generative AI are transforming cybersecurity, enabling both advanced automated defense and large-scale sophisticated attacks, with AI-generated malware predicted to account for 50% of detected threats by 2025, creating an urgent need for a holistic overview and guidance to navigate this new automated threat landscape for 2026 and beyond.

Method: The authors conducted a systematic review of over 70 academic papers, industry reports, and technical documents, synthesized insights from real-world case studies across major security platforms (Google Play Protect, Microsoft Defender, AWS, etc.) and existing initiatives, and organized analysis of dual-use applications, risks, and defensive strategies.

Result: The survey provides a structured overview of both beneficial applications (such as real-time threat detection, phishing defense, zero-day detection, DevSecOps) and malicious uses (such as AI-generated malware, large-scale vulnerability exploitation) of LLMs in cybersecurity, summarizes existing defensive approaches including the SAFE Framework and AI-driven anomaly detection, and compiles practical recommendations for responsible deployment.

Conclusion: The work establishes a benchmark for holistic cybersecurity research at the intersection of AI and threat defense, and provides a critical reference and roadmap for developing secure, scalable LLM-based cybersecurity systems for researchers, engineers, and security leaders.

Abstract: Large Language Models (LLMs) and generative AI (GenAI) systems, such as ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, LLaMA, Copilot, Stable Diffusion by OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Meta, Microsoft, Stability AI, respectively, are revolutionizing cybersecurity, enabling both automated defense and sophisticated attacks. These technologies power real-time threat detection, phishing defense, secure code generation, and vulnerability exploitation at unprecedented scales. Following a rapid surge where LLM-generated malware grew to account for an estimated 50% of detected threats by 2025, up from just 2% in 2021, navigating this highly automated threat landscape in 2026 demands next-generation security frameworks. This paper presents a comprehensive survey of the beneficial and malicious applications of LLMs in cybersecurity, including zero-day detection, DevSecOps, federated learning, synthetic content analysis, and explainable AI (XAI). Drawing on a review of over 70 academic papers, industry reports, and technical documents, this work synthesizes insights from real-world case studies across platforms like Google Play Protect, Microsoft Defender, Amazon Web Services (AWS), Apple App Store, OpenAI Plugin Stores, Hugging Face Spaces, and GitHub, alongside emerging initiatives like the SAFE Framework and AI-driven anomaly detection. We conclude with practical recommendations for responsible and transparent LLM deployment and trustworthy AI, including model watermarking, adversarial defense, and cross-industry collaboration, setting a new benchmark for rigorous, holistic cybersecurity research at the intersection of AI and threat defense, and offering a roadmap for secure, scalable LLM systems that serves as a critical reference for researchers, engineers, and security leaders navigating the complex challenges of AI-driven cybersecurity.

### [2] [Nonlinear Bandit](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07304)
*Tianshuo Zheng,Ting Wu,Zhi-Hua Zhou,Keqin Liu*

Main category: cs.LG

TL;DR: This paper studies generalized linear bandit (GLB) problems under heavy-tailed noise and piecewise constant contexts, and extends the framework to general nonlinear bandit (NB) problems, proposing a series of efficient algorithms with theoretical regret guarantees.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Heavy-tailed noise is widely observed in real-world applications including personalized recommendation, financial markets and medical treatments, and existing bandit algorithms generally lack good performance and theoretical guarantees for this scenario and general nonlinear bandit problems.

Method: 1. For GLB under heavy-tailed noise, propose the EHM algorithm based on online mirror descent (OMD), which extends the adaptive Huber loss with one-pass update (O(1) per round complexity); 2. For GLB with piecewise constant contextual characteristics, propose the revised PGLB-EHM algorithm; 3. For special and general nonlinear bandit problems, propose the NB-EHM algorithm combining the bisection method, and apply the affine lifting approach to handle general NB problems.

Result: 1. EHM achieves an almost optimal regret of Õ(T^(1/(1+ε))) with O(1) computational complexity per round, and eliminates the need to pre-know a commonly used parameter; 2. The regret upper bound order of PGLB-EHM for piecewise constant context GLB remains the same as EHM; 3. NB-EHM with affine lifting achieves a sublinear regret bound for general nonlinear bandit problems.

Conclusion: The proposed series of algorithms for heavy-tailed GLB and general nonlinear bandit problems achieve good computational efficiency and theoretically guaranteed regret bounds, solving some limitations of previous methods.

Abstract: In this paper we first study the problem of generalized linear bandit (GLB) under heavy-tailed noise. The characteristics of heavy-tailed distributions are widely observed in real-world applications such as personalized recommendation, financial markets, and medical treatments. Based on the online mirror descent (OMD) method, we propose an algorithm EHM that extends the adaptive Huber loss method (Wang et al., 2025) with one-pass update ($\mathcal{O}(1)$ computational complexity with respect to current round $t$ and the time horizon $T$), which simultaneously achieves an almost optimal regret of $\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(T^{\frac{1}{1+ε}})$ where $T$ is the time horizon. In addition, by utilizing a special property of some link function (Sawarni et al., 2025), our algorithm eliminates the need to know a commonly used parameter. Next, we study the GLB problem under the case when contextual characteristic becomes piecewise constant, and we slightly revised former algorithm to obtain the PGLB-EHM algorithm. After theoretical analysis, we prove that the regret upper bound order stays the same. Furthermore, we look deeper into a special case of nonlinear bandit (NB) and present the NB-EHM algorithm with bisection method and special restriction. Eventually we utilize the affine lifting approach and show that the general NB problem can be applied with NB-EHM to achieve a sublinear regret bound.

### [3] [A knowledge-augmented dataset of high-risk driving scenarios with LLM annotations for autonomous driving](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07103)
*Heye Huang,Jingguang Li,Zhiyuan Zhou,Paul Liang,Mingyu Wu,Kitae Jang,Jianqiang Wang*

Main category: cs.LG

TL;DR: 本文介绍了一个用于自动驾驶的知识增强高风险驾驶场景数据集K-Risk，该数据集整合了多来源轨迹数据，标注了大语言模型生成的语义信息，可为风险感知自动驾驶智能体的开发和评估提供标准化基础。


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: 自然驾驶数据中高风险尤其是罕见长尾极端场景严重不足，现有轨迹和语言增强数据集很少同时提供高风险事件标签、语义标注和可验证安全信号，无法满足自动驾驶对风险场景推理和安全决策训练评估的需求。

Method: 构建知识增强数据集K-Risk，整合来自欧美中不同道路场景的20个人类和自动驾驶轨迹数据集，采用统一的以风险为中心的提取流程整理出31398个高风险事件和1036个近碰撞极端事件子集；每个事件包含同步轨迹、元数据和大语言模型生成的三元组语义标注，代表性子集的因果风险分析和行动建议通过闭环仿真迭代验证。

Result: 得到了包含31398个高风险事件和1036个近碰撞极端事件的K-Risk数据集，每个事件配备多维度语义标注，代表性子集的标注经过仿真验证，连接了结构化交通轨迹、语义推理和决策监督。

Conclusion: K-Risk数据集结合了多维度风险标注、可解释语言监督和可验证决策，为下一代风险感知自动驾驶智能体的开发与评估提供了标准化基础。

Abstract: Safe autonomous driving requires both rapid responses to common high-risk events and deeper reasoning over rare, extreme long-tail scenarios in traffic safety. These scenarios are severely under-represented in naturalistic driving data, and existing trajectory and language-augmented datasets seldom provide high-risk event labels, semantic annotations, and verifiable safety signals. Here we present K-Risk, a knowledge-augmented dataset that combines structured driving trajectories with large language model generated semantic annotations for safety-critical driving scenarios. K-Risk integrates 20 human-driven and autonomous-vehicle trajectory datasets from Europe, China, and the United States, covering highways, urban freeways, intersections, and roundabouts. Using a unified risk-centric extraction pipeline, K-Risk curates 31,398 high-risk events, together with a 1,036-event extreme subset of near-collision cases. Each event is released as a synchronized trajectory, metadata, and language triplet containing structured scenario descriptions, abnormal-behavior notifications, and, for a representative subset, causal risk analyses and action recommendations validated through a closed-loop simulator with iterative reflection. By combining multi-dimensional risk annotations, interpretable language supervision, and verifiable decisions, K-Risk bridges structured traffic trajectories, semantic reasoning, and decision supervision, providing a standardized foundation for developing and evaluating next-generation risk-aware autonomous driving agents.

### [4] [Large Behavior Model: A Promptable Digital Twin of the Retail Customer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06993)
*Wachiravit Modecrua,Krittin Pachtrachai,Touchapon Kraisingkorn*

Main category: cs.AI

TL;DR: 本文提出了面向零售客户的大型行为模型（LBM），这是一种可提示的客户数字孪生模型，从大规模零售交易中学习客户决策过程，在多个零售任务上优于前沿通用大语言模型，可有效跨零售商、跨决策域迁移，为客户数字孪生和行为模拟提供了可扩展基础。


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: 现有客户行为建模方法要么仅优化预测准确率无法解释决策，要么模拟用户时未基于真实行为数据，因此需要一个能兼顾从真实大规模行为数据学习、可解释、可泛化的客户行为建模方案。

Method: 采用统一的人-环境（Person-Environment）框架，将客户状态表示为基于历史购买的行为画像，通过检索增强生成融入产品上下文；采用三阶段训练：对 verbalized 行为数据进行持续预训练，用于决策生成的监督微调，以及基于可验证奖励的强化学习进行校准。

Result: 在购买预测、难负例判别、购物篮补全、促销响应预测和跨域券兑换五个任务上，LBM consistently 优于前沿通用大语言模型；在跨零售商、跨决策域上表现出良好的零样本和微调迁移能力；消融实验表明持续预训练是行为泛化的主要动力，检索在训练和推理阶段同时使用时效果最好，强化学习提升了模型对显式行为证据的依赖，降低了对通用大语言模型先验的依赖。

Conclusion: 交易历史中编码的行为知识可以被大语言模型有效学习，LBM为客户数字孪生和行为模拟提供了可扩展的基础。

Abstract: Customer behavior modeling underpins recommendation, marketing, and decision support, yet existing approaches either optimize predictive accuracy without explaining decisions or simulate users without grounding them in real behavioral data. We present the Large Behavioral Model (LBM) that learns customer decision making directly from large-scale retail transactions through a unified Person-Environment formulation. Customer state is represented by a behavioral profile derived from historical purchases, while product context is incorporated through retrieval-augmented generation. The model is trained using continued pre-training on verbalized behavioral data, supervised fine-tuning for decision generation, and reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards for evidence-based calibration. We evaluate the proposed framework on purchase prediction, hard-negative discrimination, basket completion, promotion response, and cross-domain voucher redemption. The model consistently outperforms frontier general-purpose language models on in-domain retail tasks while demonstrating strong zero-shot and fine-tuned transfer across retailers and decision domains. Ablation studies show that continued pre-training is the primary driver of behavioral generalization, retrieval is most effective when applied during both training and inference, and reinforcement learning improves reliance on explicit behavioral evidence over generic language-model priors. These results demonstrate that behavioral knowledge encoded in transaction histories can be effectively learned by language models, providing a scalable foundation for customer digital twins and behavior simulation.
