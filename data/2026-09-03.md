### [1] [HypRQ-VAE: Hyperbolic Item Indexing for Long-Tail-Aware Generative Recommender Systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03369)
*Longfeng Wu,Tong Zeng,Giovanni Seni,Zhimin Peng,Bhanu Pratap Singh Rawat,Si Zhang,Yao Zhou,Lecheng Zheng,Bo Ji,Yujun Yan,Dawei Zhou*

Main category: cs.IR

TL;DR: 本文提出了HypRQ-VAE模型，通过在双曲空间学习物品索引解决生成式推荐中LLM文本令牌与离散物品索引不对齐导致的幻觉问题，同时提升长尾物品推荐效果，在公开基准数据集上验证了其有效性。


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: 当前将推荐作为大语言模型（LLM）语言建模任务的生成式推荐方法存在根本错配：LLM操作文本令牌，而推荐依赖离散物品索引，该错配容易引发推荐幻觉；现有方法在欧几里得空间学习物品词汇表，难以建模真实推荐目录固有的长尾分布，无法很好表示稀疏长尾物品。

Method: 提出双曲残差量化变分自动编码器HypRQ-VAE，这是首个在双曲空间学习物品索引的框架，利用双曲几何指数体积膨胀特性适配用户物品交互的幂律结构，在编码丰富文本语义的同时保留稀疏长尾物品的表示保真度。

Result: 在三个基准数据集上的实验表明，HypRQ-VAE显著提升了推荐性能，在长尾物品推荐上提升尤为明显；分析证明该增益来自双曲空间在生成式推荐中对物品层级结构和稀疏性的更强建模能力。

Conclusion: 在双曲空间学习物品索引能有效解决生成式推荐中LLM与离散物品索引的错配问题，同时更好适配真实交互数据的长尾分布，相比欧几里得空间的方法能显著提升推荐效果尤其是长尾物品推荐效果。

Abstract: Sequential recommender systems model user behavior as item ID sequences, while recent generative methods cast recommendation as a language modeling task using large language models (LLMs). While this paradigm incorporates rich textual semantics, it introduces a fundamental mismatch: LLMs operate on text tokens, whereas recommender systems depend on discrete item indices. This misalignment often leads to hallucinations in generative recommendations. Existing methods attempt to bridge this gap by learning item vocabularies in Euclidean space, but they struggle to model the inherent long-tail distribution of real-world catalogs, where a small number of head items dominate, and a vast number of tail items reflect users' niche preferences. To address this issue, we introduce Hyperbolic Residual-Quantized Variational AutoEncoder (HypRQ-VAE), the first framework to learn item indexing in hyperbolic space. HypRQ-VAE leverages the unique properties of hyperbolic geometry, whose exponential volume expansion naturally accommodates the power law structure of user-item interactions. This allows the model to encode rich textual semantics while preserving the representational fidelity of sparse, long-tail items. Experiments on three benchmark datasets show that HypRQ-VAE significantly improves the performance of recommendation, particularly in recommending tail items. Our analysis attributes these gains to the superior capacity of hyperbolic space to model item hierarchies and sparsity in generative recommendation. Our code and data are available at: https://github.com/wulongfeng/HypRQ-VAE.

### [2] [SelfDR: Self-Distillation from Reasoning for LLM-Based Recommendation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03313)
*Chumeng Jiang,Jiayin Wang,Xinjie Lin,Zhiqiang Guo,Hengliang Luo,Min Zhang*

Main category: cs.IR

TL;DR: 本文针对基于大语言模型（LLM）的推荐中显式生成中间推理路径计算成本高的问题，提出了基于推理自蒸馏的SelfDR框架，将推理增强的教师预测蒸馏到相同基座的学生模型实现直接推荐，在三个公开数据集上验证了该方法有效且高效。


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: LLM作为推荐的强大基座已经取得不错效果，引入推理能够帮助LLM解读丰富文本信号提升推荐准确率，但是显式生成中间推理路径会带来很高的计算成本，限制了在真实推荐系统中的实际部署，因此需要解决这个效率挑战。

Method: 提出了SelfDR自蒸馏框架，所有组件都基于同一个基座LLM，不需要外部模型：1）构建教师推荐器，以最终下游性能为奖励训练推理器，生成目标合理据并整合到教师输入中；2）相同基座的学生推荐器用于直接推荐，通过带动态加权策略的自蒸馏从教师那里学习知识。

Result: 在三个公开数据集上进行的大量实验验证了SelfDR在推荐效果、合理性和推理效率上都有提升，既提升了推荐效果又保持了推理效率。代码已公开。

Conclusion: SelfDR框架能够在不依赖外部模型的前提下，将LLM自身推理增强的预测知识蒸馏得到可直接生成推荐的学生模型，有效平衡了推荐精度和推理效率，适合实际部署，是LLM推荐中平衡效果和成本的有效方案。

Abstract: Large Language Models (LLMs) have recently emerged as powerful backbones for recommendation. To better elicit their capabilities, reasoning has been widely incorporated to help LLMs interpret rich textual signals and improve recommendation accuracy. However, explicitly generating intermediate reasoning traces often incurs substantial computational costs, which limits practical deployment in real-world recommender systems. To address this challenge, we propose SelfDR, a Self-Distillation from Reasoning framework for LLM-based Recommendation. SelfDR distills an LLM's own reasoning-enhanced predictions to produce recommendations directly, improving recommendation effectiveness while maintaining inference efficiency. All components in the framework are built on the same base LLM, without relying on any external models. Specifically, the teacher recommender is constructed by training a reasoner with downstream performance as the reward, enabling it to generate targeted rationales that are later incorporated into the teacher's input. A student recommender for direct recommendation, with the same underlying model, then learns from the teacher through self-distillation with a dynamic weighting strategy. Extensive experiments on three public datasets validate the effectiveness, rationality, and efficiency of SelfDR. Codes are available at https://github.com/JiangDeccc/SelfDistillation.

### [3] [The Dice Roll Method: A Standardized Protocol for Repeated-Query Auditing of Large Language Model Brand Recommendations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04047)
*Dmitrij Żatuchin*

Main category: cs.IR

TL;DR: This paper proposes the Dice Roll Method, a standardized statistical protocol for repeated-query auditing of stochastic variation in large language model (LLM) brand recommendations, and validates it via reanalysis of existing studies and external pre-registered validation.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: While researchers commonly use repeated identical prompts to audit stochastic variation in LLM brand recommendations, there is no existing standardized protocol for setting iteration counts, selecting stability metrics, or establishing reliability thresholds for this type of auditing.

Method: The Dice Roll Method is grounded in a generative model of temperature-scaled nucleus sampling. It decomposes total response variance into sampling, prompt-phrasing, run-to-run, and model-version components, and uses a negative-binomial mixed model for repeated measures, Cliff's delta as distribution-free effect size, dependence-preserving bootstrap, simulation-based power, generalizability-theory decomposition, and drift diagnostics on pinned snapshots. The method is tested via reanalysis of five existing brand-recommendation auditing studies and pre-registered external validation on three independent corpora.

Result: Three iteration count tiers for different use cases are identified: exploratory (n=5, G=0.58), confirmatory (n=10, G=0.74), and rigorous (n=15, G=0.81). Four families of metrics (count, set, embedding, fairness-adjusted PASOR) are found to be complementary, supporting the use of a compact metric battery instead of single indicators. External validation reproduced the D-study reliability prediction in 37 of 39 cells with no failures, and matched the n=5 power value to two decimals, though fixed iteration tiers did not transfer across settings, supporting a pilot-then-adjust approach.

Conclusion: The Dice Roll Method provides a statistically principled framework for repeated-query auditing of LLM brand recommendations that accommodates the conditional, non-Gaussian structure of real autoregressive LLM generation.

Abstract: Background: Researchers increasingly use repeated identical prompts to audit stochastic variation in large language model (LLM) brand recommendations, yet no standardized protocol exists for setting iteration counts, selecting stability metrics, or establishing reliability thresholds. Objective: We formalize the Dice Roll Method as a reusable protocol for repeated-query auditing of LLM brand recommendations, grounded in a generative model of temperature-scaled nucleus sampling. Methods: Total response variance is decomposed into sampling, prompt-phrasing, run-to-run, and model-version components. The stack: a negative-binomial mixed model with iterations as repeated measures; Cliff's delta as the distribution-free effect size; dependence-preserving bootstrap; simulation-based power; a generalizability-theory decomposition; drift diagnostics on pinned snapshots. We reanalyse five brand-recommendation auditing studies: approximately 190,000 observations, 270+ brands, 6 languages, iteration counts 5 to 40. Results: Three tiers of iteration guidance emerge from the D-study: exploratory (n = 5, G = 0.58), confirmatory (n = 10, G = 0.74), and rigorous (n = 15, G = 0.81), tied to effect-size and generalizability targets. The four metric families (count, set, embedding, fairness-adjusted PASOR) are complementary, motivating a compact metric battery over single indicators. A pre-registered external validation on three independent corpora (Motoki et al., 100-round; Rozado, 24 models; llm-stability) reproduces the D-study reliability prediction in 37 of 39 cells with no failures and the n = 5 power value to two decimals; the fixed tiers do not transfer, supporting a pilot-then-solve reading. Conclusion: The protocol gives repeated-query auditing of LLM brand recommendations a statistically principled footing under the conditional, non-Gaussian structure of real autoregressive generation.

### [4] [EPIC: Explicit Posterior Item Conditioning for Semantic ID Diffusion Recommendation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03522)
*Tuan-Binh Tran,Thanh Tam Nguyen,Quoc Viet Hung Nguyen,Dung D. Le,Tung Kieu,Thanh Trung Huynh*

Main category: cs.IR

TL;DR: This paper proposes EPIC, an explicit posterior item conditioning method for semantic ID diffusion-based generative recommendation, that improves existing masked diffusion methods by adding item-level competition to the denoising process, and obtains consistent performance improvements on Amazon benchmark datasets.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Existing masked diffusion methods for semantic ID generative recommendation conduct position-wise token predictions, but a partial semantic ID can map to multiple feasible full items during denoising, and existing methods do not account for the constraint of selecting among complete catalog items, leading to suboptimal generation results.

Method: The proposed EPIC method introduces explicit item-level competition into semantic ID denoising. It constructs a personalized posterior distribution over feasible candidate items based on the current generation context and user's recent interactions, then projects this distribution back to unresolved semantic ID positions to guide subsequent token generation. The pretrained backbone is kept frozen and no additional decoder forward pass is required.

Result: Experiments on four Amazon benchmarks show that EPIC achieves consistent performance improvements over strong baseline methods. Diagnostic analyses show that the performance gains mainly come from personalized transition evidence that preserves promising item hypotheses during the denoising process.

Conclusion: Explicitly modeling item-level posterior conditioning in semantic ID diffusion recommendation improves generation and recommendation performance by preserving promising item candidates during denoising, without adding extra overhead to the pretrained backbone.

Abstract: Semantic ID (SID) generative recommendation predicts the next item by generating a short tuple of discrete tokens. Recent masked-diffusion methods improve this process through bidirectional context and flexible decoding, yet recommendation ultimately requires selecting among complete catalog items. At each denoising step, a partial SID can correspond to multiple feasible items, while existing methods primarily reason through position-wise token predictions. We propose Explicit Posterior Item Conditioning (EPIC), which introduces explicit item-level competition into SID denoising. EPIC constructs a personalized posterior over feasible candidate items using the current generation context and the user's recent interactions, then projects this distribution back to unresolved SID positions to guide subsequent token decisions. The pretrained backbone remains frozen and requires no additional decoder forward pass. Experiments on four Amazon benchmarks show consistent improvements over strong baselines, while diagnostic analyses indicate that the gains primarily arise from personalized transition evidence that preserves promising item hypotheses during denoising.

### [6] [SENTINEL-RL: Offloading Topological Reasoning from LLM Agents in the Security Operations Center](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04159)
*Uday Vallabhaneni,Cassie L. Cagwin,David J. Wild*

Main category: cs.AI

TL;DR: This paper introduces Sentinel-RL, an agentic Security Operations Center (SOC) architecture that offloads topological reasoning from LLM agents to address scalability and consistency limitations of LLM-only autonomous SOC analysts, evaluating its performance on two real cybersecurity datasets.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: LLM agents proposed for autonomous SOC analysis have two critical flaws at enterprise scale: their finite context window cannot accommodate large multi-host authentication graphs, and free-form LLM generation cannot guarantee that containment actions are consistent with the network topology. This motivates a decoupled architecture that separates topological reasoning from LLM semantic reasoning.

Method: Sentinel-RL decouples topological reasoning from semantic reasoning: 1) a heterogeneous graph attention encoder summarizes the live authentication subgraph into a fixed-dimensional state; 2) a Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) reinforcement learning policy maps the state to a constrained set of investigative actions; 3) an LLM agent only consumes the policy's recommendations to generate analyst-readable outputs, gated by a critic. The system is evaluated on the LANL cybersecurity events dataset and Indiana University Quartz HPC cluster.

Result: 1) The two-phase CREATE ingestion pattern loads a 24M-edge authentication subgraph 24x faster than the standard MERGE-based pipeline (14.2 minutes on a single 32-core node); 2) The sliding-window alert engine meets the 25-event/10-second threshold within <=2.5s across 50 trials; 3) PPO converges to 8.74+/-0.31 mean episodic return, achieving 0.91 precision and 0.87 recall on held-out red-team events; 4) The full detect-investigate-recommend-approve loop completes in a median 6.3s. The work also contributes reusable engineering and deployment patterns.

Conclusion: Decoupling topological reasoning from LLM semantic reasoning via the Sentinel-RL architecture addresses the key limitations of LLM-only SOC agents at enterprise scale, achieving high performance, efficiency, and enterprise readiness for autonomous cybersecurity operations.

Abstract: Large language model (LLM) agents are increasingly proposed as autonomous SOC analysts, but two limitations make them unreliable at enterprise scale: a finite context window cannot hold a multi-thousand-host authentication graph, and free-form generation offers no guarantee that a recommended containment action is consistent with the topology it operates on. We present Sentinel-RL, an agentic-SOC architecture that decouples topological reasoning from semantic reasoning: a heterogeneous graph attention encoder summarizes the live authentication subgraph into a fixed-dimensional state, a Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) policy maps this state to a constrained set of investigative actions, and an LLM agent loop is restricted to consuming the policy's recommendations and producing analyst-readable narratives gated by a critic. We instantiate the system on the LANL Comprehensive, Multi-Source Cyber-Security Events dataset and the Indiana University Quartz HPC cluster, reporting four results: (i) a two-phase CREATE ingestion pattern loads a 24M-edge authentication subgraph into Neo4j in 14.2 minutes on a single 32-core node, roughly 24x faster than the canonical MERGE-based pipeline; (ii) a sliding-window alert engine reliably trips a 25-event/10-second threshold in <=2.5 s across 50 trials; (iii) PPO training over 200 iterations converges to a mean episodic return of 8.74+/-0.31, with held-out precision of 0.91 and recall of 0.87 on labeled red-team events; and (iv) the integrated containment loop completes a full detect-investigate-recommend-human-approve cycle in a median of 6.3 s. We contribute a reusable engineering pattern (the hot-node deadlock workaround), a portable HPC deployment pattern (anchor-node co-location), and an enterprise-readiness analysis covering false-positive economics, reversibility guarantees, audit compliance, and the human-approval boundary.

</details>
