### [1] [SentryLine: Evidence-Grounded Question Answering over Evolving Documents in Oncology Care](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08364)
*Tampu Ravi Kumar,Gaurav Najpande,Muhammad Ali Khan,Kaneez Zahra Rubab Khakwani,Karan Kathuria,Shorya Azriel Moses,Yuvraj Kalia,M Bassam Sonbol,Irbaz Bin Riaz,Vivek Gupta*

Main category: cs.CL

TL;DR: This paper introduces SentryLine, a question answering system for evolving oncology living guidelines, constructs the expert-annotated ASCOBENCH benchmark, and demonstrates that SentryLine outperforms existing baselines and ASCO's own guideline assistant.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Oncology care needs to constantly integrate rapidly evolving biomedical evidence, and ASCO's living guidelines (which change dynamically across multiple versioned documents) impose new information access burdens on users, creating a need for effective question answering systems tailored to these evolving clinical documents.

Method: SentryLine uses a vectorless hierarchical Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) pipeline to retrieve relevant guideline passages, generates role-specific answers with inline citations, plus factual/temporal verification reports and update drift detection notes. The authors built ASCOBENCH, a benchmark of 405 three-turn conversations across 4 question categories with gold standard answers from clinician annotators, and evaluated SentryLine against 5 baselines using an LLM-as-judge framework with three different generation backbones.

Result: SentryLine achieved consistent performance improvements over four retrieval baselines and ASCO's official guideline assistant, with especially large gains for reasoning and role-specific questions that require multi-hop synthesis and register adaptation.

Conclusion: SentryLine is an effective evidence-grounded question answering system for evolving oncology living guidelines, and the ASCOBENCH benchmark provides a valuable resource for future research in this area.

Abstract: Oncology care operates at constant pressure of absorbing rapidly evolving evidence base in biomedicine. The American Society of Clinical Oncology (ASCO) addresses this through living guidelines, but the format introduces a new burden: any recommendation can change at any point, across multiple versioned documents. We present SENTRYLINE, a living guideline-aware clinical question answering system. SENTRYLINE retrieves guideline passages through a vectorless hierarchical RAG pipeline and returns a role-specific answer with inline citations, factual and temporal verification reports, and drift detection notes that surface when a guideline has been updated. We construct ASCOBENCH, a benchmark of 405 three-turn conversations across four question categories with gold answers from expert annotators(clinicians), and use test set to evaluate SENTRYLINE against five baselines under an LLM-as-judge framework. Experiments across three generation backbones show consistent improvements over four retrieval baselines and ASCO's guideline assistant, with particularly strong gains on Reasoning and Role-Specific questions where multi-hop synthesis and register adaptation are required

### [2] [FINALLY: A Dataset Recommender System for Recommender-Systems Research](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08941)
*Louis Owie*

Main category: cs.IR

TL;DR: 本文介绍了面向推荐系统研究的数据集推荐系统FINALLY，这是一个用于构建可配置离线推荐系统评估数据集集合的网页工具，作者通过实验验证了其技术一致性和策略有效性。


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: 数据集选择决定了推荐系统算法评估的实验条件，但现有工具难以支持构建同时满足实验约束和集合级选择目标的完整数据集集合，存在功能局限性。

Method: 开发了基于网页的FINALLY数据集推荐系统，支持指定必需数据集、候选池限制、元数据过滤、可配置目标集合大小，整合了随机选择，以及基于改进有效协方差和凸包目标的多样性与非多样性选择策略；并通过在10种不同配置下进行420次推荐运行对系统进行评估。

Result: 所有生成的数据集集合都满足目标大小、去重、快照成员、必需数据集和元数据过滤要求；所有40种确定性策略-配置组合都可复现；基于有效协方差和凸包的策略都得到了预期的多样性-非多样性分数排序；多样性策略在对应目标下的分数高于全部30次随机结果，非多样性策略分数低于全部30次随机结果，验证了FINALLY工作流的技术一致性。

Conclusion: FINALLY工作流具备技术一致性，实现的策略在研究的配置空间内符合预期的优化方向，但实验并未证明生成选择的科学适用性、全局最优性或实践优越性。

Abstract: Dataset selection shapes the empirical conditions under which recommender-system algorithms are evaluated, yet existing tools provide limited support for constructing complete dataset sets that jointly satisfy experimental constraints and set-level selection objectives. To address this problem, I developed FINALLY, a web-based dataset recommender for constructing configurable dataset sets for offline recommender-systems evaluations. FINALLY combines required datasets, candidate-pool restrictions, metadata filters, configurable target-set sizes, Random selection, and diverse and non-diverse strategies based on adapted Effective Covariance and Convex Hull objectives. I evaluated FINALLY through 420 recommendation runs across ten systematically varied configurations. All evaluated dataset sets satisfied the applicable target-size, duplicate-avoidance, snapshot-membership, required-dataset, and metadata-filter requirements. All 40 deterministic strategy--configuration combinations were reproducible. Both the Effective-Covariance-based and Convex-Hull-based strategies produced the expected diverse-versus-non-diverse score ordering in all ten configurations. Under their corresponding objectives, the diverse strategies produced scores above all 30 configuration-specific Random results, whereas the non-diverse strategies produced scores below all 30 Random results. These results establish technical consistency for the evaluated FINALLY workflow and show that the implemented strategies follow their intended optimization directions within the investigated configuration space. They do not establish the scientific suitability, global optimality, or practical superiority of the generated selections.

### [3] [Agentic ML Exploration (A-MLE) for Ads Ranking](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08248)
*Erwin Gao,Vinodh Kumar Sunkara,Jingyi Guan,Qinjin Jia,Hangjun Xu,Xiang Ji,Sherman Wong,Surya Teja Chavali,Pratik Vaishnavi,Aryan Pandhi,Xiaoyu Deng,Zhaodong Wang,Samarth Inani,Fan Yang,Jakob Moberg,Zoe Zu,Nicolas Bievre,Sami Khenissi,Amit Jaspal,Ehsan Fakharizadi,Srinidhi Viswanathan,Dorothy Sun,Abishek Vanam,Sneha Iyer,Sheela Yadawad,Wenjie Chen,Gaby Nahum,Junhua Gu,Peter Chu,Yucheng Liu,Xin Zhao,Vitor Cid,Chaorong Chen,Vijay Pappu,Ashwin Kumar,Wenlin Chen,Ben Schulte,Deepak Chandra,Ritwik Tewari*

Main category: cs.AI

TL;DR: This paper introduces Agentic ML Exploration (A-MLE), an autonomous large language model (LLM) agent system for automating ML iteration cycles across multiple industrial ads ranking models, and evaluates its performance and capabilities across different LLM families after real deployment.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Modern industrial ads ranking systems are bottlenecked by low throughput of human ML iteration (each cycle takes days to weeks of senior engineer work per model, and effective techniques diffuse slowly between different models), leaving a lot of potential performance improvements unexploited.

Method: A-MLE decomposes ML exploration into 5 stages: hypothesis generation, exploration strategy, experiment execution, result analysis, and shared knowledge substrate. It is orchestrated by an LLM agent that calls domain-specific skills and workflows via a sandboxed execution layer, with human-in-the-loop checkpoints at each stage boundary. The system is evaluated with a tiered capability framework, and a controlled cross-LLM comparison is conducted across Claude Sonnet, Gemini, and GPT families.

Result: The A-MLE system is deployed on a set of large-scale ads ranking models. Cross-LLM comparison reveals qualitative differences in execution reliability and exploration aggressiveness between different LLM families. The work also discusses common failure modes and design choices that affect system reliability.

Conclusion: Agentic ML exploration is a practical force multiplier for ML engineers working on industrial recommendation/ads ranking systems, especially for long-tail models that rarely get expert attention.

Abstract: Modern industrial ads ranking stacks are increasingly bottlenecked not by model capacity or training compute, but by the throughput of human ML iteration - the cycles of research, implementation, training, debugging, evaluation, and launch required to surface a single statistically significant improvement. A typical ranking stack contains numerous differentiated models with heterogeneous data, architectures, and infrastructure constraints, and each cycle takes days to weeks of senior engineer attention per model. As a result, techniques that have proven effective on one model diffuse into others slowly and unevenly, leaving substantial recoverable signal unexplored. We present Agentic ML Exploration (A-MLE), an autonomous LLM-agent system that systematically explores ML techniques across a portfolio of ads ranking models. A-MLE decomposes ML iteration into five stages involving hypothesis generation, exploration strategy, experiment execution, result analysis and shared knowledge substrate which are orchestrated by a single agent that invokes domain-specific skills and agentic workflows against a sandboxed execution layer, with human-in-the-loop checkpoints at each stage boundary. We deploy A-MLE across a representative set of large-scale ads ranking models and evaluate it along a tiered capability framework (tool availability, autonomous workflow execution, and open-ended exploration). We further report a controlled cross-LLM study using a fixed agent loop, which surfaces qualitative differences in execution reliability and exploration aggressiveness across the Claude Sonnet, Gemini, and GPT families. We discuss failure modes and the design choices that govern reliability. Our findings suggest that agentic exploration is a practical force multiplier for ML engineers in industrial recommenders, especially for the long tail of models that rarely receive expert attention.

</details>
