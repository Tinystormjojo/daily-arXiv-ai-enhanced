### [1] [TMallGS: Scaling Unified Feature and Sequence Modeling for Generative E-commerce Search](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13398)
*Zhentao Song,Yufeng Gao,Xing Fang,Jing Wang,Guangxin Song,Bokang Wang,Yipin Dai,He Guo*

Main category: cs.IR

TL;DR: 本文提出了面向天猫搜索的可扩展排序架构TMallGS，解决了现有Transformer架构适配排序任务时忽略特征异构性的问题，通过多组件优化设计，离线实验和线上A/B测试均验证了其在训练吞吐量、搜索效果和平台交易额上的提升。


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: 工业搜索排序系统中，点击率预测正从传统深度学习模型转向计算密集型Transformer架构，以提升FLOPs利用率并通过缩放定律获得可预期收益，但现有方法在适配大语言模型架构时采用全token化策略，忽略了排序特征的异构性，因此需要针对性的可扩展架构设计。

Method: 提出TMallGS可扩展排序架构，包含五个核心组件：1）分层分布校准分词，结合域显著性重加权和分布校准投影将异构特征映射到优化子空间；2）域自适应门控Transformer主干，通过按域QKV投影和噪声自适应门控优化语义交互；3）解耦FiLM晚融合保留显式高频信号；4）上下文感知偏置网络将系统偏置与用户意图解耦；5）误差感知渐进训练，使用动态加权损失实现鲁棒学习。

Result: 在天猫搜索的大量离线实验和线上A/B测试显示，TMallGS提升了训练吞吐量，并且在单位点击转化率（UCTCVR）和商品交易总额（GMV）上取得了显著提升。

Conclusion: TMallGS是面向电商搜索排序的可扩展统一特征序列建模架构，有效解决了现有Transformer方案忽略特征异构性的问题，在真实工业场景中验证了效果增益，可应用于大规模电商搜索排序任务。

Abstract: In industrial search and ranking systems, Click-Through Rate (CTR) prediction is shifting from traditional Deep Learning Recommendation Models (DLRM) toward unified, compute-intensive Transformer architectures. This transition is driven by the need to improve Model FLOPs Utilization (MFU) and achieve predictable gains through scaling laws. However, existing approaches such as OneTrans and Climber often adopt an all-in-tokenization strategy when adapting Large Language Model (LLM) architectures, overlooking the heterogeneous nature of ranking features. We propose TmallGS, a scalable ranking architecture for Tmall search. TmallGS includes five key components: (1) Hierarchical Distribution-Calibrated Tokenization, which combines Field-wise Saliency Reweighting (FSR) and Distribution-Calibrated Projection (DCP) to map diverse features into optimized subspaces; (2) a Field-Adaptive Gated Transformer Backbone with per-field QKV projections and noise-adaptive gating for refined semantic interaction; (3) Decoupled FiLM Late Fusion to preserve explicit high-frequency signals; (4) a Context-Aware Bias Net to decouple systemic bias from user intent; and (5) Error-Aware Progressive Training with dynamically weighted losses for robust learning. Extensive offline experiments and online A/B tests on Tmall Search show that TmallGS improves training throughput and achieves substantial gains in UCTCVR and GMV.

### [2] [Optimal and Efficient Contextual Combinatorial Semi-bandits with General Function Approximation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13686)
*Hao Qin,Chicheng Zhang*

Main category: cs.LG

TL;DR: This paper studies the contextual combinatorial semi-bandit (CCSB) problem with general reward function approximation, proposes an efficient algorithm called SquareCB.Comb, and proves it achieves minimax optimal regret, outperforming prior work in generality while matching existing regret guarantees.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Existing algorithms for contextual combinatorial semi-bandits either impose restrictive structural assumptions on action sets, are limited to specific problem settings like slate recommendation, or do not achieve minimax optimal regret when using general reward function approximation, creating a need for a more general and efficient algorithm with optimal regret guarantees.

Method: The authors propose SquareCB.Comb, a computationally efficient algorithm that solves a convex optimization problem at each round to sample a combinatorial action balancing exploration and exploitation. The algorithm only requires a cardinality bound on combinatorial actions (no additional structural assumptions on the action set) and works with general reward function approximation.

Result: SquareCB.Comb is proven to achieve a minimax optimal regret bound of $O(\sqrt{m A T \log |\mathcal{F}|})$. In the realizable setting, this bound matches the state-of-the-art regret guarantees of policy search-based algorithms for the more restricted slate recommendation setting, while the proposed algorithm generalizes to arbitrary combinatorial action structures and general reward approximation.

Conclusion: SquareCB.Comb is an efficient, general algorithm for contextual combinatorial semi-bandits with general function approximation that achieves minimax optimal regret, combining broader applicability with state-of-the-art regret performance.

Abstract: We study the contextual combinatorial semi-bandit (CCSB) problem with general reward function approximation. At each round, the learner observes a context, selects a combinatorial action consisting of a subset of basic arms, and receives the reward of each selected arm; the goal is to maximize the cumulative reward over time. We propose SquareCB.Comb, a computationally efficient algorithm that, at each round, solves a convex optimization problem to sample a combinatorial action that balances exploration and exploitation. SquareCB.Comb scales to large arm sets and imposes no structural assumptions on the action set beyond a cardinality bound of $m$ on each combinatorial action. We prove that SquareCB.Comb achieves a minimax optimal regret bound of $O(\sqrt{m A T \log |\mathcal{F}|})$, where $A$ is the number of arms, $m$ is the maximum number of arms in a combinatorial action, $T$ is the time horizon, and $\mathcal{F}$ is the reward function class. In the realizable setting, this bound matches the state-of-the-art regret guarantees achieved by policy search-based algorithms in the more restricted slate recommendation settings, while simultaneously generalizing to arbitrary combinatorial action structures and general reward function approximation.

### [3] [Clustering algorithms for multivariate wind farm SCADA data filtering](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13544)
*Nicolò Italiano,Vasilis Pettas,Tuhfe Göçmen,Nicolaos A. Cutululis*

Main category: cs.LG

TL;DR: This paper compares the filtering accuracy of multiple clustering algorithms for filtering multivariate SCADA data from wind farms against expert manual filtering, tests the methods on real offshore wind farm turbine data, provides recommendations for model selection and calibration, and evaluates performance with new unlabeled-data-suitable metrics.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Wind farm SCADA systems collect large datasets containing many anomalies, transients, non-normal operational modes and hidden outliers, and only normal operation measurements are needed for most applications. Existing automated filtering methods need to be evaluated against the gold-standard manual filtering, and appropriate evaluation metrics for unlabeled SCADA data are lacking.

Method: Multiple clustering algorithms are compared for filtering accuracy against manual expert filtering. New evaluation metrics suitable for unlabeled data and robust across different applications are introduced. The algorithms are tested on 10-minute multivariate SCADA data collected from three turbines of an operating offshore wind farm that contains non-evident outliers from field tests.

Result: Cluster-based methods can detect both obvious and subtle outliers, and achieve higher filtering accuracy than manual filtering in most cases. Filtering accuracy and the amount of retained normal data vary significantly across different clustering models. The results show that extending analysis beyond power curves in both feature selection and evaluation metric design is important.

Conclusion: Clustering-based filtering outperforms manual filtering overall, but performance varies by model. Expert involvement is still required, though much less than for fully manual filtering. Recommendations are provided for generalizing model calibration to different datasets, and use cases for different clustering models are discussed.

Abstract: During wind farm operation, Supervisory Control and Data Acquisition (SCADA) systems record numerous anomalies, transients, and specific operational modes, leading to large datasets. However, for a wide range of applications, only measurements corresponding to normal operation are required and, therefore, the SCADA data must be filtered. For this purpose, several methods have been proposed to automate and replace manual filtering conducted by experts via visual inspection of the data. In this paper, we compare the filtering accuracy of multiple clustering algorithms against manual filtering, introducing evaluation metrics that are suitable for unlabeled data and robust across potential applications. Based on the results, we provide recommendations for generalizing model calibration to different datasets and discuss potential use cases for each model. The models are applied to the SCADA data of three turbines of an existing offshore wind farm, using 10-minute statistics across multiple data channels. In addition to the anomalies and operational modes typically recorded, the dataset presents a large number of non-evident outliers due to several field tests. Overall, the results highlight the importance of extending the analysis beyond the power curve, both in feature selection and in the design of evaluation metrics. In most cases, cluster-based methods are able to detect both evident and subtle outliers, achieving higher accuracy than manual filtering. However, the accuracy and the amount of data retained vary considerably depending on the model, and expert involvement remains necessary, though to a reduced extent compared to manual filtering.

</details>
