### [1] [OneLatent: Latent Reasoning for Efficient Foundation Recommendation Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30675)
*Hao Jiang,Peiru Du,Pengfei Yao,Mengting Li,Siyuan Lou,Kuo Cai,Sheng Yu,Qiang Luo,Jian Liang,Ruiming Tang,Fei Pan,Peng Jiang,Wenwu Ou*

Main category: cs.IR

TL;DR: This paper proposes OneLatent, an efficient latent reasoning framework for foundation recommendation models (FRMs) that compresses explicit Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning into learnable latent tokens, outperforming existing methods on public and industrial datasets and achieving significant revenue lift in real-world online A/B testing.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Existing explicit CoT reasoning for LLM-based FRMs incurs high inference overhead and relies on manually designed templates that cannot effectively capture diverse and dynamic user interests, which motivates this work to develop a more efficient and adaptive reasoning framework.

Method: OneLatent compresses explicit reasoning traces into learnable latent tokens for latent reasoning without verbose trace generation. It includes: 1) Multi-View Adaptive CoT (MV-ACoT) that generates high-quality diverse teacher supervision from multiple perspectives and adapts reasoning complexity per instance; 2) A three-stage latent-token alignment paradigm to internalize CoT traces into latent tokens based on pretrained FRMs; 3) A multistage curriculum-based post-training strategy to activate latent reasoning for downstream tasks.

Result: Experiments on industrial-scale Kuaishou dataset and public Kuaishou LLM-Rec benchmark show OneLatent outperforms explicit CoT-based methods and traditional baselines, improving SID@64 by 17.44% and 9.33% compared to Think and No-Think variants of FRMs respectively, with over 17x higher online inference throughput. Online A/B testing in Kuaishou advertising scenario shows 9.6% revenue lift over strong baselines.

Conclusion: OneLatent is an efficient latent reasoning framework for FRMs that achieves better performance than existing explicit CoT methods with much lower inference overhead, and delivers significant business gains in real-world production deployment.

Abstract: Large language models (LLMs) have demonstrated strong reasoning capabilities, motivating their use as the backbone of foundation recommendation models (FRMs). Existing methods enhance recommendations through explicit Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning under a Think-then-Answer paradigm. However, explicit CoT incurs substantial inference overhead by generating lengthy reasoning traces and relies on manually designed templates that struggle to capture diverse, dynamic user interests. We propose OneLatent, an efficient latent reasoning framework that compresses explicit reasoning traces into several learnable latent tokens, enabling Latent-Reason-then-Answer inference without generating verbose traces. OneLatent first introduces Multi-View Adaptive CoT (MV-ACoT), which creates diverse, high-quality teacher-generated supervision by exploring user interests from multiple perspectives and automatically adapting reasoning complexity to each instance. Building on pretrained FRMs, it then uses a three-stage latent-token alignment paradigm to progressively internalize CoT traces into learnable latent tokens. Finally, a multistage curriculum-based post-training strategy activates latent-token reasoning for downstream recommendation tasks. Experiments on an industrial-scale Kuaishou dataset and the public Kuaishou LLM-Rec benchmark show that OneLatent consistently outperforms explicit CoT-based methods and traditional baselines. Compared with the Think and No-Think variants of FRMs, OneLatent improves SID@64 by 17.44% and 9.33%, respectively, while achieving over 17x higher online inference throughput. We further develop a production serving system for scalable, real-time FRM inference. An online A/B test in Kuaishou's local-services advertising scenario shows that deploying OneLatent with this system yields an estimated 9.6% revenue lift over strong online baselines, including OneRec and OneReason.

### [2] [PAIR: Preference-Aligned Intent Retrieval for Premium People Recommendation at LinkedIn](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30941)
*Zhongkai Shangguan,Yanbin Kang,Yihan Cao,Ruijie Jiang,Qiannan Yin,Chang Yue,Zijian Zhou,Girish Kathalagiri,Changshuai Wei,Zhengming Xing*

Main category: cs.IR

TL;DR: 本文提出了面向领英高级人才推荐的偏好对齐意图检索框架PAIR，解决了职业社交网络中基于用户画像相似度的通用嵌入不适用于互补意图匹配的问题，在领英的实际检索任务上取得了有效提升。


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: 职业社交网络中，用户交互是目标驱动的跨角色交互，依赖互补意图匹配而非相似性或社交邻近性；传统基于用户画像相似度或共享连接等社交信号的推荐方法更适合好友导向社交网络，会错误排序候选人，不适用于职业平台场景。

Method: 提出偏好对齐意图检索框架PAIR，核心是对用户意图偏好而非画像相似度建模：受直接偏好优化启发，将偏好学习实例化为基于嵌入的曝光条件多负样本对比损失，无需显式参考模型即可简化训练并扩展到大规模候选集；通过带位置编码聚合用户点击历史捕捉时序顺序，实现固定时间窗口内用户表示的常量成本更新，仅需轻量CPU操作即可高效更新个性化表示，服务时无需重复LLM推理。

Result: 在领英Premium检索任务上进行评估，0.6B参数的模型取得了0.45的MAR@100。

Conclusion: PAIR框架可以有效建模职业社交网络中的互补用户意图，训练高效可扩展，服务时能低成本更新用户个性化表示，在实际专业平台推荐任务上取得良好效果。

Abstract: General-purpose embeddings trained on profile similarity often misrank candidates in professional networks, where relevance is inherently complementary rather than symmetric. For example, job seekers usually engage with recruiters rather than peers with similar credentials. Current user recommendation systems are often built on profile similarity or social signals such as shared connections, which are effective in friend-oriented social networks where users tend to connect with similar people or within existing social circles. However, this does not hold in professional platforms like LinkedIn, where interactions are often goal-driven and cross-role interactions, requiring matching based on complementary intents rather than similarity or social proximity. In this paper, we propose Preference-Aligned Intent Retrieval, a retrieval framework that is designed to model user intent preference instead of profile similarity. Our approach is inspired by the preference-learning perspective of Direct Preference Optimization, but instantiates preference learning as an exposure-conditioned multi-negative contrastive objective over embeddings. This formulation simplifies training and scales efficiently to large candidate sets without requiring an explicit reference model. Furthermore, we incorporate user interaction history by aggregating click histories with positional encoding to capture temporal order, which enables constant-cost updates of user representations over a fixed history window, allowing new interactions to be incorporated without re-encoding history. As a result, personalization can be updated efficiently using lightweight CPU operations without repeated LLM inference during serving. We evaluate PAIR on LinkedIn's Premium retrieval task. A 0.6B parameter model achieves a MAR@100 of 0.45.

### [4] [The Fusion Tax: A Diagnostic Analysis of When Hybrid Retrieval Hurts](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30612)
*Aaditya Chauhan,Arun Agarwal*

Main category: cs.IR

TL;DR: 本文对融合检索（hybrid retrieval）的弊端进行诊断分析，提出了"融合税（fusion tax）"的概念，发现在大量查询上融合检索的表现反而不如其中单个表现更好的检索器，并基于稀疏- dense检索器的性能平衡给出了融合决策的指导方案。


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: 现有研究普遍认为融合稀疏 lexical检索器和 dense神经检索器的混合检索能带来整体性能提升，但很少有研究关注融合对单个查询带来的性能损失，本文旨在填补这一空白，分析融合检索何时会损害性能。

Method: 在标准实验设置下（Anserini BM25、5个不同参数规模和世代的dense编码器、SPLADE、交叉编码器第二阶段，7个BEIR数据集，3种融合函数），通过对比融合检索与单个最优组件检索器在单个查询上的性能，量化融合带来的性能损失（融合税），分析融合税产生的场景和影响因素，最后给出融合决策指导。

Result: 即使是最优的融合方法，在13%-48%的查询上表现不如两个组件中更好的那个，在8%-21%的查询上不如仅用dense检索器；六个语料库在损失厌恶风险敏感评估下融合税都很显著；融合税大多是排序效应，保留了整体召回率，在仅被单个检索器覆盖的查询上更易出现，RRF在dense检索更强时融合税更高；融合失败仅能弱预测，跨语料迁移性差；交叉编码器仅能在自身可靠的场景下恢复部分融合税。

Conclusion: 混合检索存在不可忽视的融合税，即融合后单个查询性能反而下降的现象，应当根据目标语料库中稀疏检索和dense检索的性能平衡来决定是否进行融合，而非盲目使用融合检索。

Abstract: Hybrid retrieval, which fuses a sparse lexical retriever with a dense neural one, is widely recommended in the software documentation surveyed in September 2026, usually with reciprocal rank fusion (RRF), on the strength of aggregate gains. We ask what fusion costs on individual queries, treating "do not fuse" as a first-class outcome, in the setting in which hybrid retrieval is deployed: a standard Anserini BM25, five dense encoders spanning 22M-596M parameters and three model generations, a learned-sparse (SPLADE) alternative, and a cross-encoder second stage, on seven BEIR datasets and three fusion functions. Even the best method, dense-weighted score interpolation, ranks relevant documents below the better of its two components on 13-48% of queries and below the deployable dense-only retriever on 8-21% (14-48% and 11-25% at a weight selected under leave-one-corpus-out validation), while beating dense-only in aggregate on every corpus with the primary encoder; the downside is significant under loss-averse risk-sensitive evaluation on six of the seven corpora. We call this loss the fusion tax. It is mostly a ranking effect that preserves recall in aggregate, it is most likely on the queries that only one retriever covers, and it persists across the encoder ladder and the SPLADE substitution. RRF carries the higher tax wherever the dense retriever is clearly stronger and only beats dense-only where the two components are balanced. Fusion failure is weakly predictable within a corpus but transfers unreliably across corpora, because the features that predict it change sign with the sparse-dense balance. A cross-encoder recovers part of the tax only where it is itself reliable. We distill these findings into guidance that keys the fusion decision to the measured sparse-dense balance of the target corpus.

### [5] [LLM Multi-Agent Systems Are Vulnerable to Herding and Runaway Cascades](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30769)
*Farnoosh Hashemi,Shiyu Ji,Joice Chen,Michael W. Macy*

Main category: cs.AI

TL;DR: This paper investigates whether large language model (LLM) multi-agent systems are vulnerable to herding behavior and runaway cascades when agents make recommendations influenced by others' choices, and finds they are far more susceptible than human populations.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: LLMs are increasingly deployed in multi-agent systems where agents respond to others' decisions, creating endogenous feedback loops that may be vulnerable to social influence, herding, and runaway cascades, and this vulnerability has not been empirically tested for LLM populations.

Method: Following the design of the classic Music Lab experiment, the authors tested large populations of LLM agents recommending unfamiliar songs, comparing conditions with and without exposure to cumulative recommendation counts from other agents, and contrasted results with human behavior from the original experiment.

Result: Exposure to others' recommendations led to runaway cascades, producing extreme, unpredictable collective recommendations that diverged increasingly from the songs' intrinsic appeal. LLM populations were generally far more susceptible to herding behavior than humans, and LLM cascades were less able to self-correct compared to human cascades.

Conclusion: LLM multi-agent systems have a meaningful vulnerability to herding and runaway cascades, which has important implications for their use in recommender systems, automated evaluation, complex problem-solving, and the emergence of unauthorized conspiracies.

Abstract: Large language models (LLMs) increasingly operate in multi-agent systems where they collaborate by responding to others' choices, creating endogenous feedback loops with vulnerability to social influence, herding behavior and runaway cascades. Following the design of the classic Music Lab experiment, we tested this vulnerability in large populations of LLM agents that recommended unfamiliar songs, with or without exposure to cumulative recommendation counts. Exposure led to runaway cascades that generated extreme and unpredictable collective recommendations increasingly divergent from a song's intrinsic appeal. Compared to humans, LLM populations were generally far more susceptible to herding behavior, and unlike humans, LLM cascades were less able to self-correct. This vulnerability has implications for multi-agent collaboration in recommender systems, automated evaluation, complex problem-solving, and unauthorized conspiracies.

Abstract: We focus on optimisation-based Decision Support Systems in real-world applications. ### [7] [Major Revision, Minor Accountability: LLMs as Reviewers of Published Articles in Top-Tier Journals](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31414)
*Bastien Le Guellec,Su Hwan Kim,Raphael Bentegeac,Aghiles Hamroun*

Main category: cs.AI

TL;DR: This paper evaluates the performance of four large language models (LLMs) as reviewers for 200 published top-tier journal articles, finding that LLMs give overly harsh, inconsistent reviews with low specificity and low sensitivity for detecting intentional flaws, concluding they are not reliable for independent peer review currently.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The traditional peer review system is strained by growing scientific output, with increasing difficulty recruiting reviewers and longer turnaround times. While LLM-assisted peer review has been proposed as a solution, it remains unknown how LLMs would evaluate already published top-tier journal articles (the gold standard of current scientific validity).

Method: The authors constructed a corpus of 200 2026 CC BY 4.0 licensed articles from 20 top general-science, life-science, and clinical medicine journals, asked 4 current LLMs (2 proprietary, 2 open-weight) to produce blinded independent peer reviews. They also tested model performance on manuscripts doctored with deliberate planted field-specific methodological/statistical flaws, analyzed review decisions, consistency, and flaw detection rates.

Result: 75.6% (605/800) of LLM reviews recommended major revision or rejection, with large inter-model variation (17.5% to 100% harsh recommendation rate, p<0.001), showing strong inconsistency. For manuscripts with intentional flaws, flaw detection rates ranged from 17.0% to 60.5% across models (p<0.001). LLMs showed low specificity (falsely recommending revision for valid published papers) and low sensitivity (missing intentional flaws).

Conclusion: Current LLMs produce uneven, model- and framing-dependent peer review signals, and are not reliable as independent reviewers of scientific articles currently.

Abstract: With the growth of scientific output, the peer review system is approaching a breaking point: reviewers are increasingly hard to secure, and turnaround times lengthen accordingly. LLM-assisted or LLM-generated peer review has been proposed as a remedy, but how such models would treat manuscripts already published in top-tier journals, the closest thing the current system has to a gold standard of scientific validity, is not known. Recommending revision for papers that had already passed editorial review (low specificity) while missing flaws deliberately placed in front of them (low sensitivity), these models produce output that should be read for what it is: an uneven, framing- and model-dependent signal.
