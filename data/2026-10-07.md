### [1] [Training with Missed Targets in Generative Recommendation: Separating Supervision from Probability Competition](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10124)
*Xuesi Wang,Yangbin Shi,Xiaolin Zheng*

Main category: cs.IR

TL;DR: This paper investigates the problem of training generative recommenders when observed user-item interaction targets are missed in the initial candidate set before reranking. It disentangles the different effects of appending missed targets to training lists, finds that probability competition between appended training-only targets and original inference candidates harms ranking performance, and proposes an improved loss that removes this harmful competition, leading to significant performance gains.

Motivation: When generative recommenders generate a limited candidate set for reranking, observed interaction targets are often omitted from the initial candidates. The common training strategy of appending these missed targets to the training list mixes three different effects: changing the weight of originally retrieved targets, adding extra supervision for appended missed targets, and forcing the two groups to compete for probability mass. This confounding makes it impossible to identify the source of performance changes, so there is a need to disentangle these effects to understand how appending missed targets affects final ranking performance.

Method: The authors constructed three matched loss functions that fix the weight of originally retrieved targets, and separately introduce appended target supervision and probability competition between groups. One intermediate loss normalizes the two groups separately during training to prevent training-only appended targets from competing with original inference candidates. They conducted experiments on the released OneRec model and Amazon dataset trained generators to evaluate the effect of probability competition.

Result: Experiments confirm that probability competition between appended training targets and original inference candidates harms the ranking quality of returned items. On four Amazon Video Games experiments, removing the competition improved FT-NDCG by 7.8% to 22.2%, with statistically significant improvements. A conservative development set selection rule avoids performance loss by automatically selecting whether to use appended target training per generator, preventing a 1.7% average loss in the tested setting.

Conclusion: Probability competition between training-only appended missed targets and original inference candidates harms generative recommendation ranking performance. Candidate completion via appending missed targets should not be applied automatically, and should be evaluated for each generator individually.

Abstract: Generative recommenders return a limited candidate set and may omit observed targets before reranking. A training strategy appends these missed targets to reranker training lists, although inference still ranks only original candidates. This operation simultaneously changes retrieved-target weight, adds supervision over appended targets, and makes the two groups compete for probability. An append/no-append comparison therefore cannot explain changes in returned-item rankings. We construct three matched losses that hold retrieved-target weight fixed while introducing appended-target supervision and group competition separately. The intermediate loss trains within both groups but normalizes them separately, preventing training-only targets from competing with inference candidates. Experiments with a released OneRec model and locally trained Amazon generators show that this competition can harm returned-item ranking. In four prespecified Amazon Video Games comparisons, removing it improved full-target normalized discounted cumulative gain (FT-NDCG) by 7.8--22.2\%; 95\% intervals over users and three of four intervals over training runs excluded zero. A conservative development-set rule selected appended-target training for two of three generators in one held-out category and rejected it for all three in another, avoiding a 1.7\% loss. Candidate completion should therefore be evaluated for each generator rather than applied automatically.
