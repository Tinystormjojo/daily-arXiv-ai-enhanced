### [1] [One Polluted Page Is Enough: Evaluating Web Content Pollution in Generative Recommenders](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13610)
*Minghao Luo,Liang Chen*

Main category: cs.CL

TL;DR: This paper evaluates the vulnerability of search-augmented generative recommenders to web content pollution (e.g., fake reviews, promotional pages designed to mislead recommendations), introduces a benchmark called FORGE for testing, tests multiple LLMs, and evaluates existing defense strategies.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Search-augmented large language models (LLMs) are increasingly used for consumer recommendations by retrieving live web content, but this brings new risks that LLMs may consume polluted web content and become unwitting promoters of fake products. There was a lack of systematic evaluation of this vulnerability before this work.

Method: The authors propose the FORGE benchmark, which locally rewrites real products in real retrieved web pages into fake products to simulate controlled web content pollution, and measures the frequency of LLMs recommending fake products. FORGE covers 225 real-world products across 15 categories and 5 consumer scenarios. Experiments are conducted on 12 commercial and open-weight LLMs, and three common defense strategies (skepticism prompting, consensus filtering over model priors, and consensus filtering over cross-document evidence) are evaluated.

Result: All tested 12 LLMs are vulnerable to web content pollution: a single polluted page can lead to a fooled rate up to 27%, and replacing the top-3 search results increases the fooled rate to 73.8%. Vulnerability is higher when models lack stable prior knowledge of the products. Reasoning does not mitigate vulnerability, instead it often generates spurious social proof to justify false recommendations. Among defenses, skepticism prompting can exacerbate vulnerability, while filtering approaches risk suppressing legitimate products.

Conclusion: Generative recommenders augmented with web search are highly vulnerable to web content pollution, even just one polluted page can cause a high rate of fake product promotion. Existing defense strategies have significant drawbacks, and more effective solutions are needed for this new security risk. The FORGE benchmark is publicly released to facilitate future research.

Abstract: Search-augmented LLMs increasingly mediate everyday consumer recommendations by retrieving live web content. This creates a new risk: generative recommenders may consume polluted web content, such as fake reviews and promotional pages crafted to mislead recommendations. We ask: to what extent do search-augmented LLMs become unwitting promoters of fake products when consuming polluted retrieval results? To answer this, we introduce FORGE (Fake Online Recommendations in Generative Environments), a benchmark for measuring fake-product promotion under controlled web-content pollution. Given an upstream search result, FORGE locally rewrites real products in retrieved web pages into fake ones to simulate web-content pollution, and measures how often the LLM recommends the fake product. FORGE covers 225 real-world products across 15 categories and 5 consumer scenarios. Across 12 commercial and open-weights LLMs, all models are vulnerable: a single polluted page yields fooled rates of up to 27%, while the full top-3 replacement raises this to 73.8%. Vulnerability varies substantially across categories, increasing when models lack stable prior knowledge of the relevant products. Reasoning does not mitigate this vulnerability; instead, it often generates spurious social proof to justify false recommendations. We evaluate three defenses: skepticism prompting and consensus filtering (over model priors or cross-document evidence). Skepticism can exacerbate vulnerability, much like reasoning, while filtering risks suppressing legitimate products. We release FORGE at https://github.com/leoluolol/forge-benchmark.

### [2] [MIDSim: Simulating Multi-Channel Information Diffusion in Social Media with LLM-Powered Multi-Agent System](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13140)
*Lexi Liu,Qi Cao,Yuanhao Liu,Huawei Shen,Xueqi Cheng*

Main category: cs.SI

TL;DR: This paper proposes MIDSim, an LLM-powered multi-agent system for simulating multi-channel information diffusion in social media that accounts for both social and algorithmic diffusion channels. It constructs three real-world diffusion datasets and shows the proposed framework outperforms baselines in simulating realistic macro diffusion phenomena and generating diverse user comments.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Modeling and simulation of information diffusion in social media is important for understanding public opinion and collective behavior. Existing methods mostly focus only on diffusion via social links, ignore algorithm-driven diffusion channels (such as those from recommender systems), and fail to capture the complexity of user behavior, creating a need for more realistic simulation approaches.

Method: An LLM-powered multi-agent system is proposed, where large language models instantiate personalized user agents, and the diffusion process jointly models exposure from both social connections and algorithmic recommendation channels. Three real-world diffusion datasets covering Sina Weibo, RedNote, and Twitter are constructed, including diffusion records, user profiles, historical posts, and social relationships.

Result: Experiments on real diffusion events demonstrate that the proposed framework can realistically simulate macro-level diffusion phenomena and generate diverse user comment content, achieving significantly better performance than existing baseline methods.

Conclusion: The LLM-powered multi-agent framework MIDSim effectively addresses the limitations of traditional information diffusion simulation by incorporating multi-channel diffusion and user complexity, outperforming baselines on real-world datasets.

Abstract: Information diffusion in social media shapes public opinion and collective behavior, making its modeling and simulation an important research problem. Existing studies have investigated information diffusion through epidemic-based, cascade-based, and point process models. However, they predominantly focus on diffusion through social links, overlooking other diffusion channels enabled by platform algorithms (e.g., recommender systems) and failing to capture user behavioral complexity. To address these limitations, we propose an LLM-powered multi-agent system for simulating multi-channel information diffusion, where large language models instantiate personalized user agents and the diffusion process jointly models social and algorithmic exposure streams. We further construct three real-world diffusion dataset spanning Sina Weibo, RedNote, and Twitter, containing diffusion records, user profiles, historical posts, and social relationships. Experimental results on real diffusion events show that our proposed framework realistically simulate macro diffusion phenomenon and generate diverse comment content, significantly outperforming baselines.

### [3] [CFALR: Collaborative Filtering-Augmented Large Language Model for Personalized Fashion Outfit Recommendation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13001)
*Yujuan Ding,Junrong Liao,Yunshan Ma,Yi Bin,Wenqi Fan,Tat-Seng Chua,Qing Li*

Main category: cs.IR

TL;DR: This paper proposes CFALR, a novel framework that combines collaborative filtering (CF) with large language models (LLMs) for personalized fashion outfit recommendation, and verifies its superior performance on public benchmarks.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Personalized outfit recommendation needs to balance user preferences and aesthetic compatibility. Traditional collaborative filtering suffers from data sparsity and struggles with complex user-item-outfit relationships, while existing template-based approaches are limited by rigid pre-designed structures, creating a need for better solutions.

Method: CFALR synergizes collaborative filtering with large language models: it represents user-outfit interactions as natural language, uses LLMs to capture fashion semantics, and incorporates CF-enhanced embeddings to align semantic space and collaborative interaction space. It includes an LLM-specific architecture for outfit recommendation, a CF-augmented generative mechanism for exploring large outfit combination spaces, and trainable projection layers to integrate relational and content features.

Result: Experiments on the Polyvore and IQON public benchmarks show that CFALR outperforms both traditional CF-based and existing LLM-based methods on two tasks: personalized fill-in-the-blank and personalized outfit generation.

Conclusion: CFALR, which integrates collaborative filtering with large language models, achieves state-of-the-art performance for personalized fashion outfit recommendation, addressing the limitations of prior methods.

Abstract: Personalized outfit recommendation poses a significant challenge in e-commerce and social media platforms, requiring systems that balance user preferences with aesthetic compatibility. Collaborative filtering (CF) provides a traditional solution for this, but it struggles with data-sparse scenarios and complex user-item-outfit relationships. Meanwhile, existing template-based approaches are constrained by rigid pre-designed structures. To bridge these research gaps, we introduce CFALR (Collaborative Filtering-Augmented Large Language Model for Recommendation), a novel framework that synergizes collaborative filtering with large language models for personalized outfit recommendation. Specifically, CFALR describes user-outfit interactions in natural language and leverages LLMs to capture fashion semantics while employing CF-enhanced embeddings to bridge the semantic space and the collaborative interaction spaces. Our technical contributions include: (1) the first LLM-based architecture specifically designed for personalized outfit recommendation, (2) a CF-augmented generative mechanism that efficiently navigates the extensive combination space of outfit items, and (3) trainable projection layers that optimally integrate relational and content features. Experiments on Polyvore and IQON benchmarks demonstrate CFALR's superior performance over both traditional CF-based and LLM-based methods in personalized fill-in-the-blank and personalized outfit generation tasks.

</details>
