### [1] [Structuring and Tokenizing Distributed User Interest Context for Generative Recommendation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.20554)
*Ruizhong Qiu,Yinglong Xia,Dongqi Fu,Hanqing Zeng,Ren Chen,Xiangjun Fan,Hong Li,Hong Yan,Hanghang Tong*

Main category: cs.IR

TL;DR: 本文针对生成式推荐中现有项目分词和用户兴趣上下文建模存在的问题，提出了统一图建模和语义分词的可扩展框架G2Rec，在线部署和公开数据集实验均验证了其性能优势。


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: 生成式推荐核心的项目分词方法难以同时有效组织并注入复杂的用户行为上下文和项目语义上下文；基于图的整合方法存在可扩展性问题或仅能利用局部图信息，语义分词方法多依赖启发式规则缺少显式监督信号，会得到不准确的语义表示，现有用户兴趣上下文建模存在这些局限，因此需要提出新方法解决该问题。

Method: 提出可扩展框架G2Rec，将整体图-based用户共同参与建模与语义分词统一到工业级生成式推荐中，在不需要真实用户兴趣标签的情况下，让推荐模型捕捉整体且语义可靠的用户兴趣原型，实现对用户行为上下文更全面准确的建模。

Result: 在不同产品界面的在线部署和公开数据集上的大量实验均表明，G2Rec性能优于现有生成式推荐方法。

Conclusion: G2Rec能够有效解决现有生成式推荐中上下文建模的缺陷，可应用于工业级大规模生成式推荐，相比现有方法有显著优越性。

Abstract: Generative recommendation is an emerging paradigm that has shown promise in industrial recommendation systems, aiming to predict users' next interactions from their historical behaviors. At the core of generative recommendation lies item tokenization, which bridges item semantics and recommendation models. However, existing methods often struggle to effectively organize and inject complex user-behavioral and item-semantic contexts into recommendation models simultaneously. On the one hand, existing graph-based integration methods, such as graph serialization and graph neural networks, either suffer from scalability issues or exploit only local graph information. On the other hand, existing semantic tokenization methods typically rely on heuristics and lack explicit supervision signals, which may lead to inaccurate or suboptimal semantic representations. To address these limitations in user interest context modeling, we propose G2Rec, a scalable framework that unifies holistic graph-based user co-engagement modeling with semantic tokenization for industrial-scale generative recommendation. Overall, G2Rec enables recommendation models to capture holistic and semantically grounded user interest prototypes without requiring ground-truth user interests, thereby providing more comprehensive and accurate modeling of user behavior contexts in industrial sequential recommendation. Online deployment across product surfaces and extensive experiments on public datasets demonstrate the superiority of G2Rec over existing methods.

### [2] [Generative Engine Optimization at Scale: Measuring Brand Visibility Across AI Search Engines](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.20065)
*Pratyush Kumar*

Main category: cs.IR

TL;DR: This paper presents a large-scale empirical study of Generative Engine Optimization (GEO), measuring brand visibility across major AI search engines for different tiers of brands, identifying key patterns in source citations and content preferences, and providing baseline data and experimental protocols for GEO.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: As users increasingly get answers directly from AI search engines instead of traditional organic search results, brands need to shift from traditional SEO to optimizing for how AI engines cite and mention them (GEO/AEO). Prior work lacks large-scale empirical baseline data on AI search brand visibility, especially for non-top-tier brands such as SMEs and startups.

Method: The author analyzed over 100,000 prompt responses across more than 100 brands tracked by Ranqo between March and May 2026, categorizing brands by stature to measure visibility differences, source citation patterns, content formats, and sentiment stability.

Result: Brand visibility forms a clear three-tier ladder: global top brands appear in 73% of relevant answers, mid-market/regional brands in 44%, and small/niche brands in only 11%. 78% of cited sources are corporate websites, with YouTube the leading non-corporate source. "Best-of" listicles are the most cited content format (21% of citations), and brand sentiment is far less stable than whether a brand is mentioned at all.

Conclusion: AI brand visibility can be systematically measured, and it varies across AI platforms and strongly by brand maturity. This work provides the first large-scale baseline for GEO research, and the author proposes seven experimental protocols to test causal effects of GEO recommendations on improving AI visibility.

Abstract: People increasingly get answers straight from AI search engines like ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, and Gemini rather than scrolling search results. Brands that once focused on search engine optimization (SEO) must now optimize for how these engines represent, cite, and recommend them -- a shift variously called Generative Engine Optimization (GEO), Answer Engine Optimization (AEO), and AI Search Visibility. We treat AEO and AI Visibility as part of GEO, and study how to measure brand visibility across AI engines: what they value when they cite a brand, which sources they rely on, and what content large language models surface. The hard case is everyone outside the already-authoritative top brands -- SMEs, D2C brands, creators, and early-stage startups.
  We analyze 100K+ prompt responses across 100+ brands tracked on Ranqo between March and May 2026. First visibility runs form a clear three-tier brand-stature ladder: global household names (e.g., Stripe, Nike) appear in 73% of relevant AI answers on their first run; established mid-market and regional brands (e.g., Olipop, Klaviyo) in 44%; niche and small brands in just 11% -- about 30 percentage points per step. When engines cite sources, about 78% go to corporate websites; among non-corporate sources YouTube leads, ahead of Reddit, editorial media, and Wikipedia. The highest-leverage page is the ranked "best-of" listicle, the most-cited content format at about 21% of all citations. Sentiment is the unstable signal: whether a brand is framed positively or negatively flips about 6.7 times more often than whether it is mentioned at all.
  These findings provide a first large-scale baseline for measuring GEO: AI brand visibility can be measured, differs by platform, and varies strongly by brand maturity. We close by proposing seven v1.1 protocols to test whether specific recommendations can causally improve AI visibility.

### [3] [Multi-Granular Attention-Driven Reinforcement Learning Framework for Web Intelligent Enhancement Systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19690)
*Navin Chhibber,Deepak Singh,Anokh Kishore,Nikita Chawla,K. Anguraj*

Main category: cs.LG

TL;DR: 本文提出了一种多粒度注意力驱动的强化学习框架MGAR-WIES，用于Web智能增强系统，整合语义图建模、注意力机制和自适应强化学习来处理异构动态Web数据，实现个性化Web服务，实验表明其准确率优于现有方法，达到80%。


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Web智能增强系统越来越依赖异构动态Web数据来提供个性化上下文感知服务，但传统机器学习、深度学习和强化学习模型在持续演化的Web环境中，在语义理解、适应性和可扩展性方面存在不足，需要更有效的方法解决这些问题。

Method: 提出MGAR-WIES框架：首先收集预处理异构Web数据（结构化、半结构化、非结构化）生成统一特征表示；将其转换为动态语义图，通过注意力增强的图嵌入建模实体及其关系，捕捉局部相关性和全局上下文依赖；然后采用自适应多智能体强化学习策略，利用注意力感知语义状态优化内容推荐、导航优化、服务适配等个性化Web动作；最后集成持续在线反馈实时更新图表示和学习策略，保证持续适应性和性能。

Result: 与现有方法相比，提出的MGAR-WIES框架在准确率指标上取得了更好的结果，准确率达到80%。

Conclusion: 整合语义图建模、多粒度注意力机制和自适应强化学习的框架能有效解决动态异构Web环境下Web智能增强系统的语义理解、适应性和可扩展性挑战，性能优于现有传统方法。

Abstract: From the past few years, web intelligent enhancement systems increasingly rely on heterogeneous and dynamic web data to deliver personalized, context-aware services. However, traditional machine learning, deep learning, and reinforcement learning models often struggle with semantic understanding, adaptability, and scalability in continuously evolving web environments. In this research, a Multi-Granular Attention-based Reinforcement Web Intelligent Enhancement System (MGAR-WIES) is proposed to address the challenges by integrating semantic graph modeling, attention mechanisms, and adaptive reinforcement learning. Initially, heterogeneous web data comprising structured, semi-structured and unstructured sources are collected and preprocessed for generating unified feature representations. These representations are transformed into a dynamic semantic graph, where entities and their relationships are modeled by using graph embeddings enhanced by attention mechanisms for capturing both local relevance and global contextual dependencies. Subsequently, an adaptive multi-agent reinforcement learning strategy leverages the attention-aware semantic states to optimize personalized web actions like content recommendation, navigation optimization, and service adaptation. Finally, the continuous online feedback is further integrated to update graph representations and learning policies in real time by ensuring sustained adaptability and performance. The proposed MGAR-WIES acheived better results in terms of accuracy (80%) when compared with existing approaches.
