### [1] [RPCBench: A Benchmark for Proactive Premise Critique in LLM-based Recommendation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00918)
*Zhongru Chen,Yuan Wu,Yi Chang*

Main category: cs.CL

TL;DR: This paper introduces RPCBench, a new benchmark for evaluating the ability of LLM-based interactive recommender assistants to detect and handle faulty premises in user recommendation requests, evaluates 11 LLMs on it, and identifies key bottlenecks and empirical patterns.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: LLMs are widely used as interactive recommendation assistants, requiring evaluation beyond just item recommendation quality to testing their ability to recognize flawed user requests. Existing benchmarks either focus on ranking/generation/preference satisfaction, or error detection benchmarks are not grounded in recommendation-specific evidence, leaving a gap that needs to be filled.

Method: 1. Construct RPCBench, which contains evidence-grounded test instances from 5 recommendation domains covering 10 types of premise failures, each with a recommendation context and corrupted user query. 2. Design a fine-grained evaluation framework measuring four dimensions: proactive detection, error localization, post-detection handling, and evidence faithfulness. 3. Systematically evaluate 11 different LLMs on the benchmark to identify performance patterns.

Result: Evaluations show that proactive detection is the main bottleneck for recommender premise critique, and models perform worst on underspecified-premise errors. Target-critical information density is more important than redundant evidence. Longer reasoning does not monotonically improve critique quality: performance peaks at intermediate reasoning length, and overly long reasoning causes an overthinking penalty.

Conclusion: RPCBench fills the gap of benchmarking proactive premise critique ability for LLM-based recommendation assistants, and the evaluation reveals key challenges and empirical behavior patterns of current LLMs on this task, guiding future research in this area.

Abstract: Large language models are increasingly used as interactive recommender assistants. Their evaluation should therefore go beyond plausible item recommendation and test whether they can recognize flawed recommendation requests. Existing recommender benchmarks mainly assess ranking, generation, or preference satisfaction, while existing error-detection benchmarks are usually not grounded in recommendation-specific user and candidate evidence. To address this gap, we introduce RPCBench, a benchmark for evaluating Recommender-Premise Critique: the ability to detect, diagnose, and properly handle faulty premises in natural-language recommendation requests. RPCBench contains evidence-grounded test instances from five recommendation domains and covers ten types of premise failures. Each instance provides a visible recommendation context and a corrupted user query. We further design a fine-grained evaluation framework that measures proactive detection, error localization, post-detection handling strategy, and evidence faithfulness. Through a systematic evaluation of 11 LLMs, we find that proactive detection is the main bottleneck in Recommender-Premise Critique, and models perform worst on underspecified-premise errors. We also observe that target-critical information density matters more than redundant evidence, and that longer reasoning does not monotonically improve critique quality: performance peaks at intermediate reasoning length, while overly long reasoning is accompanied by an overthinking penalty. The code is available at https://github.com/ZhongruChen/RPCBench.

### [2] [Post-Training Science for Supervised Fine-Tuning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01244)
*Charles O'Neill,Mudith Jayasekara,Harry Partridge*

Main category: cs.CL

TL;DR: 本文针对监督微调（SFT）后训练阶段的各类超参数和设计选择进行了系统性的对照实验研究，在多个规模和架构的大语言模型、多个真实客户数据集上测试了不同选择的效果，并给出了带有不确定性度量的实践建议。


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: 目前每次监督微调任务都需要从头重新探索各类超参数和设计决策（学习率、批量大小、微调方式、训练轮数等），缺乏跨模型、数据集的系统性经验总结和可迁移的选择规则，需要对这些后训练阶段的关键选择进行统一的量化测量研究。

Method: 采用单变量控制的网格扫描实验方法，覆盖了Qwen3和Llama两个模型家族的密集模型和混合专家模型，包含从参数规模到235B的模型，在四个真实客户SFT数据集上分别对LoRA微调与全参数微调进行测试，针对多个核心问题（最优超参数随模型规模变化规律、LoRA与全微调的 trade-off、验证损失等指标与下游质量的相关性、后训练收益随模型和数据规模的缩放规律、训练轮数对指令遵循能力的影响、优化器对比等）进行验证，每个结论都附带不确定性度量。

Result: 实验系统测量了各类设计选择对SFT效果的影响，得到了不同场景下各类超参数和设计选择的表现规律，每个推荐结论都给出了对应的不确定性估计，厘清了多个常见设计选择的经验规律，比如不同设置下最优学习率批量大小的变化、LoRA秩和alpha的影响等。

Conclusion: 本文通过大规模系统性实验，为监督微调的后训练阶段各类关键决策提供了带有不确定性度量的可参考经验建议，填补了跨模型架构、规模和真实数据集的经验研究空白，为后续SFT实践提供了可迁移的选择依据。

Abstract: Every supervised fine-tuning run forces the same chain of decisions, such as learning rate, batch size, LoRA or full fine-tuning, how many epochs, which optimiser, and what data to feed the model. Each of these is typically rediscovered from scratch for every new model and dataset. Here we measure them under one instrument: a sweep that varies one lever at a time, and spans dense and mixture-of-experts models in two families (Qwen3 and Llama), on four real-world customer SFT datasets, for both LoRA and full fine-tuning. These datasets give a controlled testbed: each task carries an evaluation built with the customer, and its training data is produced by iterative supervised fine-tuning that refines model outputs until they pass that evaluation, so the supervised target is internally consistent and the task judge we report against is the criterion the data was built to satisfy. We ask how the optimal learning rate and batch size move with model scale, family, and data, and whether one selection rule transfers across them; what LoRA trades against full fine-tuning, and how its rank and alpha set what the adapter can learn; whether validation loss (or other metrics, such as loss landscape flatness) faithfully ranks downstream quality; whether post-training gains scale with model size and data volume, on a model ladder extended through mixtures-of-experts to 235B parameters; how many epochs to train before general instruction-following erodes; and whether a geometry-aware optimiser improves on AdamW. Each recommendation is paired with a measure of its uncertainty.

### [3] [When the Algorithm Becomes the Brand Crisis: A Sociotechnical Theory of Distributed Responsibility and Accountable Transparency](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00510)
*Mohammad Saleh Torkestani,Taha Mansouri*

Main category: cs.AI

TL;DR: This conceptual paper develops a sociotechnical process theory to explain how responsibility is assigned for AI/algorithmic failures that lead to brand crises, distinguishing between different types of AI-related adverse events and proposing an "accountable transparency" response framework.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Conventional brand crisis models cannot adequately address the question of how stakeholders assign responsibility for AI system failures, when technical causation, control and governance duties are distributed across multiple actors (AI system, developer, deployer, vendor, user) in modern AI applications like recommendation systems, chatbots and generative AI interfaces.

Method: A structured, federated scoping synthesis of verified academic and primary sources is used to develop the sociotechnical process theory.

Result: 1. The theory distinguishes AI/algorithmic incidents, AI-related organisational crises, and AI-related organisational scandals; it posits that incident configuration shapes actor-specific attribution, which influences stakeholder appraisals of capability, integrity, fairness and relationships, and public moralisation may escalate an incident to a scandal. 2. This theory reconciles conflicting existing findings: algorithm involvement can buffer negative brand reactions in some contexts, while robot/chatbot failures redirect blame to associated firms in others. 3. The theory introduces accountable transparency as a response framework combining six elements: timely notice, intelligible account, role-responsibility acknowledgement, remedy, evidence of correction, and recourse. 4. Synthesised evidence supports stronger conditional proximal inferences (about blame, trust, satisfaction, firm evaluation, communication credibility) than claims about durable outcomes (reputation, brand equity, market performance).

Conclusion: The developed sociotechnical process theory explains responsibility assignment in AI-related brand crises, resolves prior conflicting findings, and proposes an accountable transparency response framework, with evidence supporting its utility for understanding proximal stakeholder outcomes better than long-term market outcomes.

Abstract: Artificial intelligence systems increasingly enact market-facing promises through chatbots, recommendation systems, automated decisions, and generative interfaces. Their failures, misuse, and misrepresentation raise a question that conventional brand-crisis models do not fully specify: how do stakeholders assign responsibility when technical causation, customer-facing control, and governance duties are distributed across an AI system, developer, deployer, vendor, and user? This conceptual paper develops a sociotechnical process theory from a structured, federated scoping synthesis of verified academic and primary sources. It distinguishes an AI/algorithmic incident from an AI-related organisational crisis and, in turn, from an AI-related organisational scandal. The framework proposes that incident configuration shapes actor-specific attribution; attribution informs capability, integrity, fairness, and relationship appraisals; and public moralisation may, but need not, escalate an incident into scandal. The theory offers a reconciliation of findings that algorithm involvement can buffer negative brand reactions in some settings while robot and chatbot failures can redirect responsibility to an associated firm in others. It introduces accountable transparency as a proposed response configuration that combines timely notice, an intelligible account, role-responsibility acknowledgement, remedy, evidence of correction, and recourse. The evidence supports conditional, proximal inferences about blame, trust, satisfaction, firm evaluation, and communication credibility more strongly than claims about durable reputation, brand equity, or market performance.

### [4] [Position: Privacy Is a Claim, Not a Property of Synthetic Data](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01273)
*Jiachen Zhao,Antonia Januszewicz,Taeho Jung*

Main category: cs.LG

TL;DR: 这是一篇立场论文，指出机器学习社区中合成数据隐私的认知已经从「在给定假设下的残余推理风险主张」转变为「数据生成本身自带的属性」，作者认为该转变反映了社区隐私证据标准的隐性变化，通过实证分析展示了当前合成数据隐私研究的不规范问题，并呼吁将隐私视为明确的、基于证据的科学主张，建议会议 venues 要求隐私主张明确范围、可检验可辩论。


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: 当前合成数据广泛应用于隐私敏感场景，但社区错误地将隐私当成了合成数据本身自带的属性，而非需要验证的主张，隐私保证往往不明确、难验证，还对少数群体记录带来更高暴露风险，这种不规范的现状需要纠正。

Method: 作者对主流机器学习会议的近期出版物进行实证分析，梳理了当前合成数据隐私相关研究的现状，结合对隐私原则和社区标准的分析提出自身立场。

Result: 研究发现，合成数据在隐私敏感场景中被频繁使用时，通常没有明确阐述威胁模型、推理风险或可证伪的隐私主张，导致隐私保证往往是隐含的、难以验证且分布不均，稀有和少数群体记录面临更高的隐私暴露风险。

Conclusion: 隐私是一个需要证据支撑的明确科学主张，而不是合成数据固有的属性，机器学习会议应该采用新规范，要求隐私相关断言必须范围清晰、可检验、可辩论。

Abstract: Synthetic data has become a common component of machine learning research. While widely adopted, its use in privacy-sensitive contexts has quietly shifted from a claim of residual inference risk under stated assumptions to an appearance-based property inferred from data generation itself. In this position paper, we argue that this shift reflects an implicit change in community standards for what counts as sufficient privacy evidence, rather than a misunderstanding of well-established privacy principles. Drawing on an empirical analysis of recent publications across major ML venues, we show that synthetic data is frequently used in privacy-sensitive settings without explicit articulation of threat models, inference risks, or falsifiable privacy claims. As a result, privacy assurance often remains implicit, difficult to verify, and unevenly distributed, with heightened exposure for rare and minority records. We argue for treating privacy as an explicit, evidence-based scientific claim and recommend that ML venues adopt norms requiring privacy-relevant assertions to be clearly scoped, testable, and contestable.

### [5] [TGR: Advancing Industrial Recommendation from Generative-Paradigm Ranking toward Unified Generation and Reasoning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00986)
*TGR Team,Lei Cheng,Haonan Hu,Beibei Kong,Yudong Li,Zang Li,Yunsheng Pang,Hongyang Su,Jianchao Tu,Yunlong Wang,Bing Wen,Junzhang Zhu,Shaojie Zhu,Chengxiang Zhuo*

Main category: cs.IR

TL;DR: This paper introduces TGR (Tencent Generative Recommendation), a unified generative recommendation framework for industrial recommender systems that unifies generation and reasoning, including three core components (TGR-GenRank, TGR-GenRec, TGR-Reason). All components have been validated through online A/B testing and deployed in Tencent's production systems serving hundreds of millions of users, achieving significant performance improvements.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Traditional industrial recommender systems use cascaded multi-stage architectures with separately optimized models, which bring problems of limited scalability, fragmented decision making, and lack of semantic knowledge and reasoning ability. There is a need to advance industrial recommendation to the generative paradigm to address these issues.

Method: TGR is a unified generative recommendation framework that advances towards unified generation and reasoning through three coupled directions: 1) TGR-GenRank upgrades the ranking stage with CCFormer, which combines unified feature tokenization, scalable Transformer backbone, feature-field separated cross attention, subspace token mixing, and hierarchical sequence compression, while retaining per-item multi-task outputs; 2) TGR-GenRec explores end-to-end generation with two paradigms: BARGE solves item-boundary loss and semantic drift in hierarchical semantic-ID generation via item context-aware attention, hierarchical path reranking, and orthogonal dual-path decoding; HiGR performs whole-slate generation with prefix-structured semantic IDs, coarse-to-fine decoding, and listwise multi-objective alignment; 3) TGR-Reason injects offline-generated semantic-ID reason tokens into online decoding to achieve reasoning without request-time rollout.

Result: TGR is deployed across multiple Tencent production surfaces serving hundreds of millions of users: 1) CCFormer (TGR-GenRank) achieved significant gains in 5 A/B tested scenarios, and is fully launched in two scenarios, bringing +3.57% CTR and +1.71% advertising revenue; 2) BARGE improved Hit@5 by 10.2-16.9%, and after full rollout yielded +0.60% CTR and +1.70% reading time; 3) HiGR improved offline slate quality by 15.9-21.3% with 5x inference speedup, and achieved up to +1.22% watch time and +1.73% video views online; 4) TGR-Reason raised cold-start new-user Hit@1 by 477.8%, and delivered +1.75% effective consumption and +13.09% new-user exposure-to-conversion online.

Conclusion: The proposed TGR framework effectively advances industrial recommendation from generative-paradigm ranking to unified generation and reasoning, achieves substantial performance improvements in multiple core recommendation tasks and scenarios, and has been successfully deployed in real production systems at Tencent serving hundreds of millions of users.

Abstract: Industrial recommender systems typically rely on cascaded retrieval, pre-ranking, ranking, and reranking stages, whose separately optimized models limit scaling, fragment decision making, and lack semantic knowledge and reasoning. We present TGR (Tencent Generative Recommendation), an industrial framework that advances recommendation toward the generative paradigm along three coupled directions. TGR-GenRank upgrades ranking through CCFormer, which combines unified feature tokenization, a scalable Transformer backbone, feature-field separated cross attention, subspace token mixing, and hierarchical sequence compression while retaining per-item multi-task outputs. TGR-GenRec explores end-to-end generation under two paradigms: BARGE bridges item-boundary loss and semantic drift in hierarchical semantic-ID generation through item context-aware attention, hierarchical path reranking, and orthogonal dual-path decoding; HiGR performs whole-slate generation with prefix-structured semantic IDs, coarse-to-fine decoding, and listwise multi-objective alignment. TGR-Reason injects offline-generated semantic-ID reason tokens into online decoding, providing reasoning without request-time rollout. TGR is deployed across Tencent production surfaces serving hundreds of millions of users. CCFormer delivers significant gains in five A/B-tested scenarios and is fully launched in two, including +3.57% CTR and +1.71% advertising revenue. BARGE improves Hit@5 by 10.2-16.9% and yields +0.60% CTR and +1.70% reading time after full rollout. HiGR improves offline slate quality by 15.9-21.3% with a 5x inference speedup and achieves up to +1.22% watch time and +1.73% video views. TGR-Reason raises cold-start new-user Hit@1 by 477.8% and delivers +1.75% effective consumption and +13.09% new-user exposure-to-conversion online.

</details>
